Probabilistic knowledge graph embedding methods learn entity variances for uncertainty quantification, but these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}: an entity receives identical uncertainty regardless of which relation is queried.
This structural limitation prevents detection of out-of-distribution queries involving familiar entities in unfamiliar relational contexts.
We propose a semantic-structural decomposition that separates two orthogonal uncertainty types: \emph{semantic uncertainty} from learned embeddings (capturing entity frequency) and \emph{structural uncertainty} from coverage (capturing entity-relation co-occurrence).
Combining both signals achieves 0.87--0.96 AUROC on standard benchmarks, with consistent synergy across DistMult, TransE, and ComplEx architectures.
Critically, the two signals exhibit complementary failure modes: on temporal-like OOD (emerging entities), GP outperforms coverage (0.83 vs 0.78); on new entity-relation pairs, coverage achieves perfect detection while GP fails.
Under adversarial corruptions targeting each signal's weaknesses, CAGP shows positive synergy in 4/5 settings.
The primary contribution is not the combination algorithm but the decomposition framework itself, which explains why existing uncertainty methods systematically underperform on knowledge graphs.
