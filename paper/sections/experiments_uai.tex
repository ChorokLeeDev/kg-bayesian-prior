\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three static benchmarks: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14,541 entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR} (40,943 entities, 11 relations), and \textbf{YAGO3-10} (123,161 entities, 37 relations), with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits based on entity frequency and co-occurrence (Appendix~\ref{app:ood_splits}). To validate beyond simulated protocols, we also evaluate on \textbf{ICEWS14} (7,128 entities, 230 relations), a temporal KG with ground-truth timestamps where train/test splits are chronological---OOD categories emerge naturally from temporal evolution, breaking the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Score-based (UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}), ensemble (MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}), distance-aware (SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}), and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). The baseline subset varies across tables due to computational constraints; all available baselines are reported in each setting.
All methods share identical OOD splits, evaluation protocol, training hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=$1e-3, 30 epochs), and negative sampling strategy per dataset; ICEWS14 baselines use the same chronological split and categorization threshold ($\tau{=}$25th percentile).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). Statistical significance via paired bootstrap ($p < 0.01$).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
WN18RR, FB15k-237, and ICEWS14 use a unified pipeline with seeds $\{42, 123, 456\}$, 30 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.001$, $w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$. YAGO3-10 was trained separately (50 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.01$; see Appendix~\ref{app:training_config}). Per-seed breakdowns are in the supplementary material. Code and outputs will be released upon acceptance.

\textbf{Key findings:} Probabilistic baselines achieve near-random performance (0.38--0.63) across all evaluated settings---none reliably distinguish temporal distribution shift.\footnote{Our base DistMult achieves MRR/Hits@10 comparable to vanilla DistMult in the literature. OOD detection results are not sensitive to base model quality (\S\ref{app:architectures}).} The decomposition achieves 0.86--0.99 AUROC (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), substantially above all relation-agnostic baselines. Crucially, this pattern holds on ICEWS14 (CAGP: 0.99), where OOD is defined by \emph{ground-truth timestamps} rather than coverage---validating that coverage captures genuine temporal patterns, not merely a definitional artifact. Structural (coverage) signal is the primary driver on temporal OOD (0.76--0.99 AUROC), while semantic signal provides complementary value on emerging-entity slices where coverage is partial (0.59--0.84 AUROC). CAGP's learned $\alpha$ is effective when both signals contribute (YAGO3-10: $+$0.18 over $U_{\text{str}}$) but degrades on sparse-relation graphs (WN18RR: 0.72 vs $U_{\text{str}}$ 0.86; see error analysis for mitigations).

\textbf{Theorem predictions vs.\ observations.} Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} checks each part of Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} on two datasets:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part (i):} Semantic uncertainty achieves 0.49/0.40 on novel contexts (near-random, as predicted $\approx 0.5$)
\item \textbf{Part (ii):} Structural uncertainty achieves 0.78/0.83 on emerging entities (imperfect $< 1$, as predicted)
\item \textbf{Part (iii):} Structural AUROC $= 1.000$ on novel contexts across both datasets---expected by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), but novel contexts constitute 11--25\% of test triples, making this signal practically important
\item \textbf{Part (iv):} RelCondVar matches $U_{\text{str}}$ (0.86--0.94), confirming coverage drives temporal OOD detection; on YAGO3-10 (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), CAGP achieves the largest gain ($+$0.18 over $U_{\text{str}}$)
\end{itemize}

The complementarity pattern is consistent across datasets but with different signal strengths: on FB15k-237, semantic uncertainty is strong on emerging entities (0.81) while structural is weaker (0.78); on WN18RR (only 11 relations), structural is already strong on emerging entities (0.83) but semantic is weaker (0.71). YAGO3-10 shows the most balanced pattern (semantic 0.82, structural 0.76), where neither signal dominates---consistent with CAGP's largest absolute gain on this dataset ($+$0.18). The WN18RR learned-$\alpha$ limitation and mitigations are discussed in the error analysis below.

Table~\ref{tab:standard} shows standard OOD with random corruptions. Score-based methods excel (0.99) because random corruptions produce implausible triples with low prediction scores---a signal CAGP does not use. SNGP's distance-aware uncertainty (0.60--0.63) underperforms score-based methods because its GP output layer operates on entity-level features without conditioning on the specific relation, making it relation-agnostic like the methods analyzed in Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}; additionally, KG embedding spaces are not Euclidean, so distance-based uncertainty estimates lose discriminative power compared to direct score-based approaches. CAGP's 3\% gap (0.96 vs 0.99 on FB15k-237) reflects that random corruptions often substitute entities that share coverage with the original (structurally plausible but semantically wrong). CAGP remains competitive (0.87--0.96) while providing robust temporal performance that score-based methods lack entirely (0.38--0.60). In practice, the two approaches are complementary: a practitioner can threshold on prediction scores for corruption detection and on CAGP uncertainty for temporal drift, with no additional training cost.

\textbf{Different failure modes, different solutions.} Score-based methods (UKGE, Energy) detect implausible triples via low prediction confidence---this works for random corruptions ("Paris → president → Eiffel Tower") but fails for temporal shift where plausible new facts emerge. Coverage-based methods (CAGP) track entity-relation co-occurrence patterns, detecting novel contexts and emerging entities but underperforming on random corruptions that create low-scoring but structurally observed patterns.

\textbf{Practical recommendation.} Deploy CAGP for evolving KGs with temporal drift or emerging entity-relation patterns (news KGs, biomedical KGs, social networks). Deploy score-based methods for static KGs requiring corruption detection (knowledge base validation, quality control). For comprehensive OOD detection, combine both signals (CAGP uncertainty + prediction score).

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned variance ablation.}
We also evaluated a variant (RelCondVar) that replaces entity-level variance with a learned $\sigma^2(e,r) = \text{MLP}([e; r])$ while retaining coverage in the combination ($\alpha{=}0.5$). On temporal OOD, RelCondVar matches $U_{\text{str}}$ across all datasets (WN18RR: both 0.86; FB15k-237: both 0.94; ICEWS14: 0.99; see supplementary material). This confirms that the coverage signal---not the specific variance parameterization---drives temporal OOD detection. Notably, CAGP's learned $\alpha$ underperforms on WN18RR (0.72 vs 0.86) because the noisy GP signal on sparse-relation graphs degrades the combination; RelCondVar's fixed weighting avoids this failure mode.

\paragraph{Empirical vs theoretical AUROC.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} predicts semantic AUROC $= 0.5$ on novel contexts, but Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} shows 0.40--0.49. This discrepancy arises because Assumption A3 is approximate: novel-context entities tend toward higher frequencies than the ID average, causing slightly \emph{lower} semantic uncertainty than ID. The prediction remains directionally correct (near-random).

\paragraph{Binary vs continuous coverage.}
To test whether co-occurrence frequency improves upon binary presence/absence, we evaluated continuous coverage formulations (log-scaled, TF-IDF) on temporal OOD. Binary coverage achieves AUROC $= 1.0$ on FB15k-237 novel contexts (expected by construction; see Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), while continuous variants (log-scaled, TF-IDF) achieve only 0.56--0.59 (near-random). The key insight is that continuous coverage introduces frequency-based variations among observed pairs that obscure the binary observed/unobserved boundary, causing ID and OOD uncertainty distributions to overlap (Table~\ref{tab:coverage_ablation}). For emerging entities, GP variance already captures frequency through learned embeddings, making explicit frequency counts redundant.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
\emph{Temporal OOD.} On WN18RR, $U_{\text{str}}$ achieves 0.86 AUROC; the gap from 1.0 reflects emerging entities where coverage is non-zero ($\rho = 0.13$; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). CAGP's learned $\alpha$ degrades to 0.72 by over-weighting the noisy GP signal---a limitation of the normalization-based combination on sparse-relation graphs (11 relations).
Three mitigations bound this limitation: (1)~RelCondVar avoids it via fixed weighting, matching $U_{\text{str}}$ (0.86); (2)~matched-coverage analysis confirms GP provides genuine separation (0.79 AUROC on WN18RR, 0.86 on FB15k-237 when coverage is controlled); (3)~on YAGO3-10 where both signals contribute ($U_{\text{sem}}$ 0.82, $U_{\text{str}}$ 0.76), CAGP adds $+$0.18 over $U_{\text{str}}$, showing the combination works when GP variance is informative.
The contribution is the decomposition framework, not the specific $\alpha$-learning mechanism.

\emph{Standard OOD.} On FB15k-237 (random tail corruption, 20K ID + 20K OOD), CAGP achieves 0.971 AUROC.
False positives (8.7\%) occur on triples with low-degree tails (mean degree 183 vs 583 for correctly classified ID).
False negatives (8.7\%) involve corrupted tails that coincidentally create higher-degree entities (mean 183 vs 37 for typical corruptions).
See Appendix~\ref{app:error_analysis} for detailed breakdown.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
On the three static benchmarks, temporal OOD splits are simulated from entity frequency and coverage rather than ground-truth timestamps; the novel-context category is defined using the same coverage indicator as our structural detector, so structural AUROC $= 1$ on this subset is guaranteed by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).
However, on ICEWS14 (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), OOD is defined by chronological timestamps---novel contexts arise from temporal evolution, not coverage labels---and CAGP still achieves 0.99 AUROC (3 seeds, std$=$0.000), confirming the decomposition captures genuine temporal patterns.
A leakage audit (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}) confirms strict temporal ordering (no timestamp overlap), but identifies 53\% inverse-relation overlap and 28\% exact triple repetition---common in temporal event KGs. Under a \emph{strict split} that removes all exact duplicates and inverse overlaps (58.5\% of test triples removed), CAGP \emph{improves} from 0.993 to 0.995 AUROC (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}), ruling out transductive artifacts.
The non-trivial empirical finding is that novel contexts constitute 23--32\% of test triples across all four datasets. Where evaluated, existing probabilistic methods fail on them (AUROC 0.38--0.63), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.
Our relation-conditioned variance ablation (RelCondVar) matches $U_{\text{str}}$ across all datasets, confirming the decomposition framework rather than variance parameterization is the key contribution. RelCondVar provides a relation-aware proxy: it has explicit access to relation-specific variance $\sigma^2(e,r)$ via MLP, yet still requires explicit coverage for novel-context detection---consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}, which applies to \emph{any} entity-level uncertainty, including GNN-derived embeddings that aggregate existing neighborhood information but cannot signal the absence of an observation. Full R-GCN validation remains future work.

\paragraph{Non-definitional evidence.}
While structural AUROC $= 1$ on novel contexts is by construction on simulated splits (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), four lines of evidence demonstrate value beyond this definitional link:
(a)~On \emph{ICEWS14} (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), OOD is defined by ground-truth timestamps, not coverage, yet CAGP achieves 0.99 AUROC---the strongest evidence that coverage captures real temporal patterns.
(b)~On \emph{emerging entities}---where coverage coupling is weakest ($\rho = 0.13$--$0.68$; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions})---$U_{\text{sem}}$ provides 0.71--0.81 AUROC (Table~\ref{tab:complementarity}), complementing $U_{\text{str}}$ (0.78--0.83).
(c)~On \emph{standard OOD} (random corruptions, Table~\ref{tab:standard}), which uses no coverage-based labels, the decomposition achieves 0.87--0.96.
(d)~Ablating coverage on ICEWS14 (Appendix~\ref{app:icews14_ablation}): removing structural signal ($\alpha{=}1$) drops AUROC from 0.99 to 0.50; shuffling entity-relation associations drops to 0.55; random coverage at matched density drops to 0.50. The \emph{specific} entity-relation structure, not density statistics, drives detection.
(e)~\emph{Matched-coverage analysis:} Among test triples sharing the same coverage value (both endpoints covered), GP semantic uncertainty alone separates emerging from ID with 0.79 AUROC (WN18RR) and 0.86 AUROC (FB15k-237)---coverage is controlled, yet the semantic signal still discriminates, confirming the two signals are complementary.
(f)~The impossibility theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}) is independent of how OOD is defined.
