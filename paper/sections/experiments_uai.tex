\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on four benchmarks: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14,541 entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR} (40,943 entities, 11 relations), \textbf{YAGO3-10} (123,161 entities, 37 relations), and \textbf{ICEWS14} (7,128 entities, 230 relations with timestamps).
For ICEWS14, we train on Jan--Sep 2014 and evaluate on Oct--Dec 2014, providing genuine temporal distribution shift.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Score-based (UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}), ensemble (MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}), distance-aware (SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}), and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
AUROC (primary), AUPR, F1@0.5. Statistical significance via paired bootstrap ($p < 0.01$).

\paragraph{Relative contributions.}
Coverage provides the majority of gains on temporal OOD (0.824 vs 0.687 for semantic on ICEWS14). The combination adds +8\% over coverage alone. This asymmetry reflects that novel contexts dominate temporal shift.

Augmenting baselines with coverage improves them substantially (+0.18--0.26 AUROC), confirming coverage is the key signal. However, CAGP still outperforms augmented baselines by 0.08--0.09 AUROC because: (1) the GP-based semantic uncertainty is better calibrated than score-based uncertainty, and (2) the learned mixing adapts to the OOD distribution.

\paragraph{Empirical vs theoretical AUROC.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} predicts semantic AUROC $= 0.5$ on novel contexts, but Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} shows 0.42. This discrepancy arises because assumption (A3) (frequency overlap) is approximate: novel-context entities tend toward higher frequencies than the ID average, causing slightly \emph{lower} semantic uncertainty than ID. The prediction remains directionally correct (near-random).
