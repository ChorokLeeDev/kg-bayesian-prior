\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
Two static benchmarks with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14.5K entities, 237 relations) and \textbf{WN18RR} (40.9K, 11). Two temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps: \textbf{ICEWS14} (7.1K, 230) and \textbf{ICEWS18} (23K, 256), where OOD categories emerge from chronological splits---breaking the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}, MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}, GNNSafe~\citep{wu2023energy}, and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). GNNSafe uses energy-based OOD scoring on a 2-layer MLP with the same hyperparameters as other baselines; we include it to test whether GNN-style architectures escape the impossibility theorem. All share identical splits, hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=10^{-3}$, 30 epochs), and evaluation protocol. Standalone $U_{\text{sem}}$ (GPOnly) reports raw entity variance; the CAGP-internal semantic component is rescaled (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Metrics and reproducibility.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). WN18RR and ICEWS18 use 5 seeds; FB15k-237 and ICEWS14 use 10 seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:per_seed}). Per-seed breakdowns show standard deviations $\leq 0.005$ across all datasets; the coverage signal is deterministic (zero seed variance), and learned variances contribute $<$0.01 AUROC variation. \emph{Statistical significance}: On FB15k-237 (10 seeds), CAGP vs.\ $U_{\text{str}}$ yields a paired t-test $p < 0.001$ (mean diff = 0.032, 95\% CI: [0.029, 0.035]); on ICEWS14, the difference is not significant ($p = 0.18$) due to the ceiling effect. We report 95\% bootstrap CIs (10K resamples over seeds, not triples) for key comparisons in Appendix~\ref{app:significance}. Models with learned variance receive $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$); ablation in \S\ref{sec:ablations} confirms that $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ contributes $<$0.1pp. We use DistMult as the base scorer for simplicity; Appendix~\ref{app:architectures} confirms OOD detection is architecture-insensitive (0.959--0.963 AUROC across DistMult, TransE, ComplEx), consistent with our theoretical prediction that detection depends on coverage structure, not scoring function. Base DistMult achieves MRR/Hits@10 of 0.21/0.34 (FB15k-237) and 0.27/0.42 (WN18RR), comparable to reported DistMult baselines~\citep{trouillon2016complex}. We do not compare Mahalanobis distance (the OOD signal is relational, not geometric), conformal prediction (targets prediction-set guarantees, not detection), or evidential methods (targets classification, not KG triple scoring).

\textbf{Key findings} (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}).

\emph{Non-circular evidence (ICEWS14/18).} On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps, coverage dominates: $U_{\text{str}}$ achieves 0.99 AUROC on both ICEWS benchmarks, and CAGP matches or exceeds this. On ICEWS14 (10 seeds), CAGP reaches 0.99 overall AUROC with 1.00 on emerging entities---a +2pp emerging gain over $U_{\text{str}}$ alone. On ICEWS18, CAGP performs identically to $U_{\text{str}}$ across all OOD categories due to a ceiling effect. These results confirm that \emph{structural uncertainty is the primary missing ingredient}---the gap between baselines (0.34--0.87) and coverage (0.99) is the core finding.

\emph{Diagnostic evidence (static benchmarks).} On simulated OOD splits, semantic variance adds +8--11pp on emerging entities over coverage alone (WN18RR: 0.91 vs 0.83; FB15k-237: 0.89 vs 0.78). The mixture-AUROC decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}) serves as a \emph{consistency check}: predicted and observed AUROC match within 0.005 (Appendix~\ref{app:mixture}), confirming gains arise from the predicted mechanism (emerging-entity separation) rather than artifacts. Novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}); these results test the decomposition's \emph{internal structure}, not independent detection capability.

\emph{RelCondVar.} Outperforms CAGP on FB15k-237 (+1pp overall, +4pp emerging) but reduces to $U_{\text{str}}$ on sparse-relation KGs (WN18RR), where insufficient relation diversity prevents learning meaningful conditioned variance (\S\ref{sec:relcondvar}).

\emph{Why does semantic help on static but not temporal?} On ICEWS14/18, nearly all emerging entities have zero coverage for the test relation (68\% of ICEWS14 emerging entities are entirely absent from training; Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}), so $U_{\text{str}}$ already captures them---leaving no room for semantic tiebreaking. On static benchmarks (WN18RR, FB15k-237), 34--66\% of emerging entities \emph{do} have coverage for the query relation ($\rho$ in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}), creating ties that semantic uncertainty breaks. The Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}(iii) formula predicts exactly this: the gain is $\pi_e \cdot (A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{comb}} - A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{str}})$, which is positive only when emerging entities have non-trivial coverage overlap ($\rho > 0$).

\subsection{Validating Complementarity}

Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} confirms each part of Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}: $U_{\text{sem}}$ achieves near-random AUROC on novel contexts (0.34--0.40 on static benchmarks; anti-predictive due to high-frequency novel-context entities having lower variance than the in-distribution average). GNNSafe exhibits the same pattern: 0.43 on FB15k-237 novel contexts (anti-predictive), confirming the impossibility applies to GNN-style architectures that still produce entity-level uncertainties. $U_{\text{str}}$ is imperfect on emerging entities (0.78--0.83 on static; near-perfect on ICEWS where most emerging entities lack coverage). The combination helps: CAGP's gain comes entirely from emerging entities (+8--11pp over $U_{\text{str}}$), matching the mixture decomposition to within 0.005 (Appendix~\ref{app:mixture}):
\begin{equation}
\text{AUROC}(U) = \pi_e \cdot A_{\text{emerge}}(U) + \pi_n \cdot A_{\text{novel}}(U) + \delta_{\text{tie}}
\label{eq:mixture_auroc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adversarial Evaluation: ICEWS14 Strict Split}
\label{sec:strict_split}

Inverse relations (\emph{visited} in train, \emph{was\_visited\_by} in test) can inflate temporal OOD performance. We construct a \emph{strict split} removing all inverse-relation overlap (58.5\% of test triples removed).

Energy collapses to chance (0.59$\to$0.50); $U_{\text{sem}}$ degrades modestly (0.84$\to$0.82), confirming that relation-agnostic variance provides no reliable signal for temporal OOD even on temporal data---consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}. \paragraph{Sensitivity to removal fraction.}
To verify that 58.5\% is not a cherry-picked threshold, we swept removal fractions from 0\% to 100\%. Coverage AUROC remains 0.991$\pm$0.001 across \emph{all} fractions (3-seed; Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}), confirming robustness.

\subsection{When Coverage Alone Fails: Ambiguous Coverage Benchmark}
\label{sec:ambiguous_coverage}

To demonstrate that semantic uncertainty is \emph{necessary} (not merely complementary) when coverage overlap $\rho > 0$, we construct a synthetic benchmark where coverage alone cannot distinguish OOD:

\paragraph{Design.} 1000 entities split into ``common'' (30\%, high frequency) and ``rare'' (70\%, low frequency). Common entities appear with most relations in training; rare entities appear with a small subset. Test OOD consists of rare entities paired with relations they \emph{are covered for}---so $U_{\text{str}} = 0$, yet they remain OOD (low semantic confidence).

\paragraph{Results (3 seeds).} On the \texttt{emerging\_covered} subset (rare entities with coverage):

\small
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Method & AUROC \\
\midrule
$U_{\text{str}}$ & 0.69 \\
$U_{\text{sem}}$ & \textbf{1.00} \\
CAGP & 0.90 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Semantic provides \textbf{+31pp} over structural on this synthetic subset---an upper bound demonstrating that semantic uncertainty \emph{can be necessary} when $\rho > 0$. On real KGs with $\rho \in [0.3, 0.7]$, semantic gains are +8--11pp (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}). This validates Theorem~\ref{thm:info_decomp}: the conditional mutual information $I(U_{\text{sem}}; Y \mid U_{\text{str}})$ is positive precisely when coverage does not fully determine OOD status.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Margin loss.}
Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0$) causes $<$0.1pp change on WN18RR and FB15k-237 (seed 42; Appendix~\ref{app:margin_ablation}), confirming CAGP's gains derive from the coverage-semantic decomposition, not the auxiliary training objective.

\paragraph{Baseline + coverage.}
Post-hoc coverage augmentation ($0.5 \cdot U_{\text{baseline}} + 0.5 \cdot U_{\text{str}}$) does not match CAGP: on WN18RR, Energy+Cov$=$0.53, MC~Dropout+Cov$=$0.81, CoverageOnly$=$0.86, vs.\ CAGP$=$0.92. On FB15k-237, MC~Dropout+Cov$=$0.92 vs.\ CAGP$=$0.97. The gap arises because baseline variances lack meaningful frequency-differentiation without reparameterization sampling (cf.\ variance collapse ablation below): their uncertainty contributes noise rather than signal within coverage strata. CAGP's reparameterization-trained variances produce a rank-preserving tiebreaker that separates emerging entities \emph{within} the $U_{\text{str}}{=}0$ stratum (where 34--66\% of emerging entities fall; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).

\paragraph{Variance collapse.}
Omitting reparameterization sampling collapses entity variances (AUROC: 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR). Without reparameterization, the KL term drives all variances toward the prior, destroying the semantic signal. The reparameterization trick is essential.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Static novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction; these are diagnostic, not performance claims. ICEWS14 strict split breaks this coupling. ICEWS18 shows a ceiling: $U_{\text{str}}=$ CAGP across all subsets. Additional temporal benchmarks would further validate complementarity on diverse KG structures.

\paragraph{Standard OOD.}
On standard random-corruption OOD (Appendix~\ref{app:standard_ood_full}), CAGP ties the best single signal (0.82 on FB15k-237) and is marginally below $U_{\text{sem}}$ on WN18RR (0.66 vs 0.67): coverage provides no information when corruptions sample entities that already have the query relation in training. CAGP is designed for temporal OOD; standard OOD is a complementary setting where entity-level methods are sufficient.
