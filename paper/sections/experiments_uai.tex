\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three static benchmarks: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14,541 entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR} (40,943 entities, 11 relations), and \textbf{YAGO3-10} (123,161 entities, 37 relations), with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits based on entity frequency and co-occurrence (Appendix~\ref{app:ood_splits}). To validate beyond simulated protocols, we also evaluate on \textbf{ICEWS14} (7,128 entities, 230 relations), a temporal KG with ground-truth timestamps where train/test splits are chronological---OOD categories emerge naturally from temporal evolution, breaking the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Score-based (UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}), ensemble (MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}), distance-aware (SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}), and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). We omit KG2E~\citep{he2015learning} as its per-relation covariances target link prediction, not OOD detection, and its uncertainty $U = \text{KL}(p(h) \| p(r)) + \text{KL}(p(t) \| p(r))$ remains entity-level (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} applies). The baseline subset varies across tables due to computational constraints; all available baselines are reported in each setting.
All methods share identical OOD splits, evaluation protocol, training hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=$1e-3, 30 epochs), and negative sampling strategy per dataset; ICEWS14 baselines use the same chronological split and categorization threshold ($\tau{=}$25th percentile).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). Statistical significance via paired bootstrap ($p < 0.01$).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All datasets use a unified pipeline with 30 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.001$, $w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$, seeds $\{42, 123, 456\}$. Per-seed breakdowns are in the supplementary material. Code and outputs will be released upon acceptance.

\textbf{Key findings:} On emerging entities---the non-trivial detection task---CAGP achieves 0.79--0.91 AUROC on static benchmarks and 1.0 on ICEWS14, consistently outperforming the best baseline per dataset (0.49--0.85).\footnote{Our base DistMult \citep{yang2015embedding} achieves MRR/Hits@10 comparable to vanilla DistMult in the literature. OOD detection results are not sensitive to base model quality (\S\ref{app:architectures}).} Coverage alone achieves 0.67--0.83 on emerging entities; CAGP adds +8--12pp by leveraging semantic variance. Overall AUROC (0.90--0.99) reflects the additional boost from novel-context detection. On ICEWS14 with ground-truth temporal splits, CAGP (0.99) outperforms all baselines including Deep Ensemble (0.80) and SNGP (0.74), confirming that coverage captures genuine temporal patterns. All CAGP vs.\ best-baseline differences are significant ($p < 0.01$, paired bootstrap, 10K iterations).

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned scoring does not help.}
We test whether relation-conditioned scoring (bilinear $h^\top W_r t$ with basis decomposition) improves OOD detection when uncertainty remains entity-level. It does not: novel-context AUROC matches $U_{\text{sem}}$ across all three datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:bilinear}), confirming that relation-aware \emph{scoring} does not produce relation-aware \emph{uncertainty}. Adding coverage (Bilinear+CAGP) recovers performance to match standard CAGP, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.

\textbf{Theorem predictions vs.\ observations.} Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} checks each part of Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} on three datasets:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part (i):} Semantic uncertainty achieves 0.34/0.41/0.42 on novel contexts (below-random, as predicted $\lesssim 0.5$)
\item \textbf{Part (ii):} Structural uncertainty achieves 0.78/0.83/0.67 on emerging entities (imperfect $<1$, as predicted)
\item \textbf{Remark~\ref{rem:novel_perfect}:} Structural AUROC $= 1.000$ on novel contexts across all three datasets---by construction, but novel contexts constitute 11--25\% of test triples, making this signal practically important
\item \textbf{Part (iii):} Using the mixture decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}), the predicted CAGP AUROC matches observations to within 0.01 on all datasets (e.g., WN18RR: $0.83 \times 0.91 + 0.17 \times 1.0 = 0.925$, observed 0.92). The synergy formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:synergy}) correctly predicts that all improvement comes from emerging entities (+0.08/+0.11/+0.12 over $U_{\text{str}}$)
\end{itemize}

The complementarity pattern is consistent across all three datasets but with different signal strengths: on YAGO3-10, semantic uncertainty is strong on emerging entities (0.80) while structural is weaker (0.67); on WN18RR (only 11 relations), structural is already strong on emerging entities (0.83) while semantic is moderate (0.66); on FB15k-237, semantic achieves 0.62 on emerging while structural is similar (0.78). Across all datasets, semantic uncertainty fails on novel contexts (0.34--0.42). CAGP's combination successfully exploits this complementarity across all settings (see Figure~\ref{fig:complementarity} for a visual illustration on FB15k-237).

\textbf{YAGO emerging-entity analysis.} On YAGO, CAGP achieves 0.79 on emerging entities, slightly below $U_{\text{sem}}$'s 0.80. This occurs because $\alpha{=}0.5$ dilutes the strong semantic signal with a weaker structural signal on this specific category. However, the mixture decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}) explains why this is optimal overall: CAGP's emerging-entity loss ($-$0.01 vs $U_{\text{sem}}$) is far outweighed by its novel-context gain ($+$0.58 vs $U_{\text{sem}}$'s 0.42), yielding 0.90 overall vs 0.60 for $U_{\text{sem}}$ alone. The synergy formula correctly predicts this: the emerging-entity contribution $\pi_e \times (0.79 - 0.67) = 0.06$ matches the observed CAGP--$U_{\text{str}}$ gap of 0.059.

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned variance ablation.}
We also evaluated RelCondVar, which replaces entity-level variance with a learned $\sigma^2(e,r) = \text{MLP}([e; r])$ while retaining coverage ($\alpha{=}0.5$, fixed). On temporal OOD, RelCondVar matches $U_{\text{str}}$ across all datasets (WN18RR: both 0.86; FB15k-237: both 0.94; YAGO: both 0.84; ICEWS14: 0.99) but does not exceed it---the MLP-based variance provides no additional signal beyond coverage. CAGP outperforms RelCondVar on all datasets (0.92 vs 0.86 on WN18RR; 0.97 vs 0.94 on FB15k-237; 0.90 vs 0.84 on YAGO), demonstrating that reparameterization-trained entity variances provide genuine complementary information that the MLP-based variant does not capture.

\paragraph{Empirical vs theoretical AUROC.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} predicts semantic AUROC $\approx 0.5$ on novel contexts, but Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} shows 0.34--0.42. This discrepancy arises because Assumption A3 is approximate: novel-context entities tend toward higher frequencies than the ID average, causing slightly \emph{lower} semantic uncertainty than ID. The Spearman-based bound (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}) confirms these values are within the predicted range ($|\text{AUROC} - 0.5| \leq 0.24$--$0.37$).

\paragraph{Binary vs continuous coverage.}
To test whether co-occurrence frequency improves upon binary presence/absence, we evaluated continuous coverage formulations (log-scaled, TF-IDF) on temporal OOD. Binary coverage achieves AUROC $= 1.0$ on FB15k-237 novel contexts (expected by construction; see Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), while continuous variants (log-scaled, TF-IDF) achieve only 0.56--0.59 (near-random). The key insight is that continuous coverage introduces frequency-based variations among observed pairs that obscure the binary observed/unobserved boundary, causing ID and OOD uncertainty distributions to overlap (Table~\ref{tab:coverage_ablation}). For emerging entities, GP variance already captures frequency through learned embeddings, making explicit frequency counts redundant.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
\emph{Temporal OOD.} On WN18RR, $U_{\text{str}}$ alone achieves 0.86 AUROC; the gap from 1.0 reflects emerging entities where coverage is non-zero ($\rho = 0.34$; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). CAGP closes this gap: its semantic signal lifts emerging-entity detection from 0.83 to 0.91, yielding 0.92 overall.

\emph{Variance collapse without sampling.} A na\"ive implementation that omits reparameterization sampling in the scoring function suffers \emph{training collapse}: KL regularization drives all entity variances to a uniform value ($\sigma^2_e \to 1.0$, std${=}0$ across entities) since the scoring function does not use the variance parameters and provides no counter-gradient. Train-set calibration then yields $\mu_{\text{cov}}{=}0$ (all training triples are covered by construction), scaling the degenerate GP signal by ${\sim}10^{-8}$---the combined uncertainty reduces to float noise for covered triples. This collapse reduces AUROC from 0.92 to 0.72 on WN18RR and from 0.90 to 0.68 on YAGO. The fix is standard variational inference: using the reparameterization trick \citep{kingma2014autoencoding} in the scoring function ensures that the reconstruction loss (BCE) provides gradients to the variance parameters, preventing KL-driven collapse. This diagnosis applies to any probabilistic KG embedding that learns per-entity variances.

Matched-coverage analysis further confirms the semantic signal's value: among test triples sharing the same coverage value, GP semantic uncertainty alone separates emerging from ID with 0.79 AUROC (WN18RR) and 0.86 AUROC (FB15k-237).

\emph{Standard OOD.} An additional error analysis on FB15k-237 using prediction-score-based detection (20K balanced samples) yields 0.971 AUROC---higher than Table~\ref{tab:standard} because prediction scores are strong corruption detectors.
False positives (8.7\%) occur on triples with low-degree tails; false negatives (8.7\%) involve corrupted tails that coincidentally create higher-degree entities.
See Appendix~\ref{app:error_analysis} for detailed breakdown.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
On the three static benchmarks, temporal OOD splits are simulated from entity frequency and coverage rather than ground-truth timestamps; the novel-context category is defined using the same coverage indicator as our structural detector, so structural AUROC $= 1$ on this subset is guaranteed by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).
ICEWS14 (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}) breaks this coupling: OOD is defined by chronological timestamps, and CAGP achieves 0.99 AUROC. A leakage audit (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}) confirms temporal integrity; under a strict split removing all duplicates and inverse overlaps (58.5\% removed), CAGP improves from 0.993 to 0.995, ruling out transductive artifacts.
The non-trivial empirical finding is that novel contexts constitute 11--25\% of test triples across datasets (up to 25\% on FB15k-237). Where evaluated, existing probabilistic methods fail on them (AUROC 0.38--0.80), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.
Note that $U_{\text{str}}$ is equivalent to a binary frequency heuristic: $\mathbb{1}[\text{freq}(e,r) > 0]$. A continuous log-frequency variant achieves only 0.66--0.71 AUROC (near-random), confirming that binary presence/absence---not frequency magnitude---is the discriminative signal.
RelCondVar matches $U_{\text{str}}$ across all datasets but does not exceed it, while CAGP outperforms both---confirming that the learned entity variance, when properly trained via reparameterization, provides genuine complementary information. Despite having relation-specific variance $\sigma^2(e,r)$ via MLP, RelCondVar still requires explicit coverage for novel-context detection, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.

\paragraph{Non-definitional evidence.}
While structural AUROC $= 1$ on novel contexts is by construction on simulated splits (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), three lines of evidence demonstrate value beyond this definitional link:
(a)~On \emph{ICEWS14} (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), OOD is defined by ground-truth timestamps, not coverage, yet CAGP achieves 0.99 AUROC---the strongest evidence that coverage captures real temporal patterns.
(b)~Ablating coverage on ICEWS14 (Appendix~\ref{app:icews14_ablation}): removing structural signal ($\alpha{=}1$) drops AUROC from 0.99 to 0.50; shuffling entity-relation associations drops to 0.55; random coverage at matched density drops to 0.50. The \emph{specific} entity-relation structure, not density statistics, drives detection.
(c)~\emph{Matched-coverage analysis:} Among test triples sharing the same coverage value (both endpoints covered), GP semantic uncertainty alone separates emerging from ID with 0.79 AUROC (WN18RR) and 0.86 AUROC (FB15k-237)---coverage is controlled, yet the semantic signal still discriminates, confirming complementarity.
