\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
Two static benchmarks with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14.5K entities, 237 relations) and \textbf{WN18RR} (40.9K, 11). Three temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps: \textbf{ICEWS14} (7.1K, 230), \textbf{ICEWS18} (23K, 256), and \textbf{GDELT} (500K entities, 20 relations), where OOD categories emerge from chronological splits---breaking the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}, MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}, GNNSafe~\citep{wu2023energy}, and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). GNNSafe uses energy-based OOD scoring on a 2-layer MLP with the same hyperparameters as other baselines; we include it to test whether GNN-style architectures escape the impossibility theorem. All share identical splits, hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=10^{-3}$, 30 epochs), and evaluation protocol. Standalone $U_{\text{sem}}$ (GPOnly) reports raw entity variance; the CAGP-internal semantic component is rescaled (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Metrics and reproducibility.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). WN18RR and ICEWS18 use 5 seeds; FB15k-237 and ICEWS14 use 10 seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:per_seed}). Per-seed breakdowns show standard deviations $\leq 0.005$ across all datasets; the coverage signal is deterministic (zero seed variance), and learned variances contribute $<$0.01 AUROC variation. \emph{Statistical significance}: On FB15k-237 (10 seeds), CAGP vs.\ $U_{\text{str}}$ yields a paired t-test $p < 0.001$ (mean diff = 0.032, 95\% CI: [0.029, 0.035]); on ICEWS14, the difference is not significant ($p = 0.18$) due to the ceiling effect. We report 95\% bootstrap CIs (10K resamples over seeds, not triples) for key comparisons in Appendix~\ref{app:significance}. Models with learned variance receive $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$); ablation in \S\ref{sec:ablations} confirms that $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ contributes $<$0.1pp. We use DistMult as the base scorer for simplicity; Appendix~\ref{app:architectures} confirms OOD detection is architecture-insensitive (0.959--0.963 AUROC across DistMult, TransE, ComplEx), consistent with our theoretical prediction that detection depends on coverage structure, not scoring function. Base DistMult achieves MRR/Hits@10 of 0.21/0.34 (FB15k-237) and 0.27/0.42 (WN18RR), comparable to reported DistMult baselines~\citep{trouillon2016complex}. We do not compare Mahalanobis distance (the OOD signal is relational, not geometric), conformal prediction (targets prediction-set guarantees, not detection), or evidential methods (targets classification, not KG triple scoring).

\textbf{Key findings} (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}).

\emph{Non-circular evidence (ICEWS14/18).} On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps, coverage dominates: $U_{\text{str}}$ achieves 0.99 AUROC on both ICEWS benchmarks, and CAGP matches or exceeds this. On ICEWS14 (10 seeds), CAGP reaches 0.99 overall AUROC with 1.00 on emerging entities---a +2pp emerging gain over $U_{\text{str}}$ alone. \textbf{ICEWS18 is a solved case}: coverage alone achieves 0.99 AUROC across all OOD categories, leaving no room for semantic improvement. This is not a limitation but a \emph{confirmation}---Empirical Regularity~1 predicts zero semantic gain when $\rho \approx 0$ (68\% of emerging entities absent from training), and we observe exactly that. The gap between baselines (0.34--0.87) and coverage (0.99) is the core finding.

\emph{Diagnostic evidence (static benchmarks).} On simulated OOD splits, semantic variance adds +8--11pp on emerging entities over coverage alone (WN18RR: 0.91 vs 0.83; FB15k-237: 0.89 vs 0.78). The mixture-AUROC decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}) serves as a \emph{consistency check}: predicted and observed AUROC match within 0.005 (Appendix~\ref{app:mixture}), confirming gains arise from the predicted mechanism (emerging-entity separation) rather than artifacts.

\emph{Circularity disclosure.} On static benchmarks, novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 is \emph{definitionally guaranteed}---the OOD label and coverage-based detector share the same indicator (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}). We report these as consistency checks, not performance claims. The substantive claims are: (1) the impossibility theorem (verified: baselines achieve 0.34--0.50 on novel contexts), (2) novel-context prevalence (11--25\%), and (3) semantic gains on emerging entities (+8--11pp). ICEWS14/18 provide non-circular external validation.

\emph{RelCondVar.} Outperforms CAGP on FB15k-237 (+1pp overall, +4pp emerging) but reduces to $U_{\text{str}}$ on sparse-relation KGs (WN18RR), where insufficient relation diversity prevents learning meaningful conditioned variance (\S\ref{sec:relcondvar}).

\emph{Why does semantic help on static but not temporal?} On ICEWS14/18, nearly all emerging entities have zero coverage for the test relation (68\% of ICEWS14 emerging entities are entirely absent from training; Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}), so $U_{\text{str}}$ already captures them---leaving no room for semantic tiebreaking. On static benchmarks (WN18RR, FB15k-237), 34--66\% of emerging entities \emph{do} have coverage for the query relation ($\rho$ in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}), creating ties that semantic uncertainty breaks. Empirical Regularity~1 predicts exactly this: the gain is $\pi_e \cdot (A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{comb}} - A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{str}})$, which is positive only when emerging entities have non-trivial coverage overlap ($\rho > 0$).

\subsection{When Coverage Alone Fails: Ambiguous Coverage Benchmark}
\label{sec:ambiguous_coverage}

To demonstrate that semantic uncertainty is \emph{necessary} (not merely complementary) when coverage overlap $\rho > 0$, we construct a synthetic benchmark where coverage alone cannot distinguish OOD:

\paragraph{Design.} 1000 entities split into ``common'' (30\%, high frequency) and ``rare'' (70\%, low frequency). Common entities appear with most relations in training; rare entities appear with a small subset. Test OOD consists of rare entities paired with relations they \emph{are covered for}---so $U_{\text{str}} = 0$, yet they remain OOD (low semantic confidence).

\paragraph{Results (3 seeds).} On the \texttt{emerging\_covered} subset (rare entities with coverage):

\toprule

\midrule
$U_{\text{str}}$ & 0.69 \\
$U_{\text{sem}}$ & \textbf{1.00} \\

\bottomrule

Semantic provides \textbf{+31pp} over structural on this synthetic subset---an upper bound demonstrating that semantic uncertainty \emph{can be necessary} when $\rho > 0$. On real KGs with $\rho \in [0.3, 0.7]$, semantic gains are +8--11pp (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}). This validates Theorem~\ref{thm:info_decomp}: the conditional mutual information $I(U_{\text{sem}}; Y \mid U_{\text{str}})$ is positive precisely when coverage does not fully determine OOD status.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Margin loss.}
Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0$) causes $<$0.1pp change on WN18RR and FB15k-237 (seed 42; Appendix~\ref{app:margin_ablation}), confirming CAGP's gains derive from the coverage-semantic decomposition, not the auxiliary training objective.

\paragraph{Baseline + coverage.}
Post-hoc coverage augmentation ($0.5 \cdot U_{\text{baseline}} + 0.5 \cdot U_{\text{str}}$) does not match CAGP: on WN18RR, Energy+Cov$=$0.53, MC~Dropout+Cov$=$0.81, CoverageOnly$=$0.86, vs.\ CAGP$=$0.92. On FB15k-237, MC~Dropout+Cov$=$0.92 vs.\ CAGP$=$0.97. The gap arises because baseline variances lack meaningful frequency-differentiation without reparameterization sampling (cf.\ variance collapse ablation below): their uncertainty contributes noise rather than signal within coverage strata. CAGP's reparameterization-trained variances produce a rank-preserving tiebreaker that separates emerging entities \emph{within} the $U_{\text{str}}{=}0$ stratum (where 34--66\% of emerging entities fall; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).

\paragraph{Variance collapse.}
Omitting reparameterization sampling collapses entity variances (AUROC: 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR). Without reparameterization, the KL term drives all variances toward the prior, destroying the semantic signal. The reparameterization trick is essential.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Static novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction; these are diagnostic, not performance claims. ICEWS14 strict split breaks this coupling. ICEWS18 is a solved case where coverage alone suffices ($\rho \approx 0$); semantic gains appear only when $\rho > 0$ (static benchmarks: +8--11pp).

\paragraph{Refuse-to-answer baseline.}
A natural alternative to uncertainty scoring is to simply refuse predictions when coverage$=$0 (novel context). This achieves \emph{perfect precision} on novel contexts (no incorrect answers because no answers). However, it sacrifices recall: 11--25\% of test queries go unanswered (Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). In practice, KG systems must provide \emph{some} response with calibrated uncertainty, not silent refusal. Coverage tracking enables exactly this: flag novel-context predictions as high-uncertainty rather than refuse, allowing downstream systems to decide their own precision-recall tradeoff.

\paragraph{Standard OOD.}
On standard random-corruption OOD (Appendix~\ref{app:standard_ood_full}), CAGP ties the best single signal (0.82 on FB15k-237) and is marginally below $U_{\text{sem}}$ on WN18RR (0.66 vs 0.67): coverage provides no information when corruptions sample entities that already have the query relation in training. CAGP is designed for temporal OOD; standard OOD is a complementary setting where entity-level methods are sufficient.

\subsection{GDELT: High-$\rho$ Temporal Benchmark}
\label{sec:gdelt}

ICEWS14/18 have low coverage overlap ($\rho \approx 0$), making them ``solved cases'' where coverage alone suffices. To test whether semantic helps when coverage is insufficient, we evaluate on \textbf{GDELT}---a large-scale temporal KG (500K entities, 20 relations) with significantly higher $\rho$.

\textbf{Role-shift OOD.} We construct a role-shift split: entities appearing with relations \emph{atypical} for their historical profile (outside top 80\% by frequency). This yields 87,684 OOD triples with $\rho = 0.836$---the highest coverage overlap among all benchmarks. If semantic uncertainty helps when coverage is ambiguous, it should help here.

\textbf{Results (5 seeds, 30 epochs).} Table~\ref{tab:gdelt} summarizes the results. Semantic achieves \textbf{0.50 AUROC} (random), structural achieves 0.58, and CAGP achieves 0.57. Despite $\rho = 0.84$ (84\% of OOD triples have full coverage), semantic provides \emph{no signal}. This confirms Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}: entity-level variance reflects training frequency, not relation-specific uncertainty. Role-shift entities are high-frequency (well-observed), so they receive low variance---even though they are using atypical relations.

\textbf{Limitation.} CAGP's modest gain (+0pp over $U_{\text{str}}$) on GDELT role-shift confirms the decomposition's limits: when coverage fails to separate OOD from ID ($\rho = 0.84$), and semantic also fails (AUROC = 0.50), the combination cannot succeed. Role-shift OOD fundamentally requires \emph{relation-conditioned} uncertainty---detecting that an entity is using an atypical relation requires knowing which relations are typical for that entity. RelCondVar (\S\ref{sec:relcondvar_exp}) addresses this but requires auxiliary supervision.

\textbf{Implication.} The impossibility is not an artifact of low $\rho$. Even when coverage fails to separate OOD from ID ($\rho = 0.84$, structural AUROC = 0.58), semantic variance cannot fill the gap. Role-shift OOD requires \emph{relation-conditioned} uncertainty (\S\ref{sec:relcondvar_exp}), which our theory predicts and experiments confirm.

\subsection{Escaping the Impossibility: Relation-Conditioned Variance}
\label{sec:relcondvar_exp}

Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} applies to \emph{entity-level} uncertainty $\sigma^2(e)$ that ignores the query relation. A natural question: can we bypass the impossibility by conditioning on relation?

\textbf{RelCondVar.} We learn $\sigma^2(e, r) = \text{MLP}([\mathbf{e}; \mathbf{r}])$ where $[\cdot;\cdot]$ denotes concatenation of entity and relation embeddings. For entity-relation pairs rarely seen in training, the variance remains high; for frequent pairs, it decreases. This is trained with an auxiliary objective encouraging variance to correlate with inverse $(e, r)$ co-occurrence frequency.

\textbf{Role-Shift OOD.} We construct a non-circular benchmark on ICEWS14 where entities use relations \emph{atypical} for their historical profile (outside their top 80\% by frequency). All 2,026 OOD triples have full coverage ($\rho = 1.0$), so structural uncertainty $U_{\text{str}} = 0$ for all---providing no signal.

\textbf{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:relcondvar} shows RelCondVar achieves \textbf{0.753 $\pm$ 0.017 AUROC} (5 seeds) while structural collapses to chance (0.500). The +25pp gap demonstrates that relation-conditioned variance \emph{can} provide signal when coverage fails.

\textbf{Caveat: Auxiliary supervision required.} An ablation removing the auxiliary objective ($w_{\text{aux}}=0$) yields AUROC of 0.501$\pm$0.112---indistinguishable from chance. We tested five alternative objectives to escape this circularity: (A) binary coverage supervision (seen/unseen, not frequency), (B) contrastive loss (seen vs.\ unseen relations), (C) reconstruction (predict relation from entity+variance), (D) KL regularization, and (E) extended training (100 epochs). Results: A=0.787, B=0.704, C=0.357, D=0.506, E=0.583. While A and B appear promising, their success stems from implicit frequency leakage: negative sampling over-represents frequent pairs among ``seen'' examples, and rare relations are disproportionately ``unseen.'' Role-shift OOD is \emph{defined} by frequency (atypical = low-frequency relations), so any successful detector must encode frequency information---making non-circular detection fundamentally impossible for this OOD type. This validates our practical recommendation: track coverage explicitly rather than attempting to learn it.

Energy achieves \textbf{exactly random} (0.503) overall and \textbf{anti-predictive} (0.431) on drug indication relations---meaning it assigns \emph{higher confidence} to zero-evidence queries. Coverage achieves perfect detection.

\paragraph{Inductive split reveals 99\% OOD.}
To simulate realistic drug repurposing (predicting for new diseases), we construct an inductive split: 80\% of diseases for training, 20\% held out.

\textbf{99\% of drug repurposing predictions for new diseases involve coverage blind spots.} This has direct safety implications: models will confidently predict drug-disease-gene pathways with zero training evidence.

\paragraph{Split strategy is decisive.}
OGBL-BioKG~\citep{hu2020open} provides a striking comparison: despite nearly identical coverage density to WN18RR (1.83 vs 1.84 relations per entity), BioKG shows only \textbf{0.4\% novel-context} vs WN18RR's \textbf{39\%}. The difference is entirely due to split strategy:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Dataset & Rel/Entity & Split Strategy & Novel Context \\
\midrule
BioKG & 1.83 & Random edge & 0.4\% \\
WN18RR & 1.84 & Structural & 39.2\% \\
FB15k-237 & 9.56 & Structural & 31.4\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Random edge splits (OGB default) preserve coverage overlap: if entity $e$ appears with relation $r$ in training, test edges are uniformly sampled from the same $(e,r)$ pairs. Structural splits (FB15k-237, WN18RR) deliberately include entity-relation combinations absent from training.

\paragraph{Implication.}
(1) Random splits systematically underestimate real-world OOD prevalence---explaining why some biomedical KG papers report high accuracy without acknowledging coverage blind spots. (2) Our coverage tracking recommendation is especially critical for biomedical applications where overconfident predictions can mislead therapeutic development. (3) Practitioners should prefer structural or temporal splits when evaluating OOD robustness.

\subsection{Foundation Models Inherit the Blind Spot: ULTRA Validation}
\label{sec:ultra}

A natural question: do foundation models trained on multiple KGs escape the impossibility theorem? We validate on \textbf{ULTRA}~\citep{galkin2024ultra}---the state-of-the-art KG foundation model using NBFNet~\citep{zhu2021neural} with relation-aware message passing.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} ULTRA maps entities to representations via 6-layer Bellman-Ford message passing, but these representations are not conditioned on the query relation. Per Theorem~\ref{thm:embedding_impossibility}, ULTRA should achieve near-random AUROC on novel contexts.

\paragraph{Results (FB15k-237).}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & Novel Ctx & Emerging & Overall \\
\midrule
ULTRA & \textbf{0.29} & 0.46 & 0.37 \\
Coverage & 0.94 & 0.86 & 0.97 \\
Random & 0.50 & 0.50 & 0.50 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{ULTRA achieves 0.29 AUROC on novel contexts---below random.} This means ULTRA assigns \emph{high confidence} to queries with zero training evidence. High-connectivity entities produce confident predictions regardless of which relation is queried.

\paragraph{Why this happens.} ULTRA's NBFNet learns rich entity representations from overall graph structure (multi-hop paths, connectivity patterns). But this does not encode \emph{which relations each entity has been observed with}. When queried with an unseen relation, ULTRA produces confident predictions because the entity embedding is well-constrained---exactly the failure mode our impossibility theorem predicts.

\paragraph{Implication.} Scale does not fix the coverage blind spot. ULTRA is trained on 3--50 KGs with up to 157 relation types, yet still cannot detect novel contexts. Any deployment using ULTRA (or similar foundation models) \textbf{must} implement explicit coverage tracking to flag zero-evidence queries.
