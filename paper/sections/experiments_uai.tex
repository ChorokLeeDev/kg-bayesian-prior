\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three static benchmarks: \textbf{FB15k-237}~\citep{toutanova2015observed} (14,541 entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR}~\citep{dettmers2018convolutional} (40,943 entities, 11 relations), and \textbf{YAGO3-10}~\citep{mahdisoltani2015yago3} (123,182 entities, 37 relations), with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits based on entity frequency and co-occurrence (Appendix~\ref{app:ood_splits}). To validate beyond simulated protocols, we also evaluate on \textbf{ICEWS14} (7,128 entities, 230 relations) and \textbf{ICEWS18} (23,033 entities, 256 relations), temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps where train/test splits are chronological---OOD categories emerge naturally from temporal evolution, reducing the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Score-based (UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy} adapted to KGE by using the negative link-prediction score as proxy energy), ensemble (MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}), distance-aware (SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}), and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). We omit KG2E~\citep{he2015learning} as its per-relation covariances target link prediction, not OOD detection; its uncertainty $U = \text{KL}(\mathcal{N}(\mu_h{-}\mu_t,\,\Sigma_h{+}\Sigma_t) \| \mathcal{N}(\mu_r,\Sigma_r))$ uses fixed per-relation-type Gaussian parameters, not per entity-relation co-occurrence indicators, so the entity frequency-matching argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} applies to the entity terms. All baselines are evaluated on all datasets. Note: standalone $U_{\text{sem}}$ (GPOnly) reports raw entity embedding variance $(h_{\text{var}} + t_{\text{var}})/2$ without any normalization; this differs from the CAGP-internal semantic component, which is rescaled by training-data statistics (see \S\ref{sec:method}).
All methods share identical OOD splits, evaluation protocol, training hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=10^{-3}$, 30 epochs), and negative sampling strategy per dataset; ICEWS14/ICEWS18 baselines use the same chronological split and categorization threshold ($\tau{=}$25th percentile).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). We also report calibration diagnostics (ECE, Brier) and selective prediction in the appendix. Note that AUPR is identical for CAGP and $U_{\text{str}}$ across all datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}): in high-precision retrieval, coverage dominates and the semantic signal adds no lift---CAGP's advantage over $U_{\text{str}}$ is AUROC-specific (recall-weighted).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All datasets use a unified pipeline with 30 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.001$, $w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$, seeds $\{42, 123, 456\}$ (5 seeds for WN18RR; 10 seeds for FB15k-237; CAGP multi-seed stds: WN18RR 0.005, FB15k-237 overall 0.001/emerging 0.002, confirming stability). Per-seed breakdowns are in the supplementary material. Code and outputs will be released upon acceptance.
\textbf{Training signal asymmetry:} Models with learned variance (GPOnly, CAGP, RelCondVar) receive an uncertainty margin loss ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$) encouraging higher uncertainty on negative samples; baselines without variance parameters (UKGE, Energy, CoverageOnly) receive only BCE loss. This reflects an architectural constraint, not a selective advantage: the margin loss is undefined for parameter-free uncertainty signals. See Appendix~\ref{app:margin_ablation} for an ablation on the effect of this loss.
Although $\alpha$ is parameterized as a learnable logit, the link prediction objective provides zero gradient to it (all training triples have $U_{\text{str}}{=}0$), so it remains at its initialization of 0.5 across all datasets and seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity}).

\textbf{Key findings:} The decomposition framework predicts that combining semantic and structural signals should achieve 0.79--0.91 AUROC on emerging entities and near-perfect detection on novel contexts. ICEWS14 provides the primary non-circular validation: structural coverage is the strongest signal (both CAGP and $U_{\text{str}}$ reach $\sim$0.99 overall), but the semantic term adds a genuine gain on residual emerging entities ($U_{\text{str}}$: 0.98 $\to$ CAGP: 1.00, +2pp). ICEWS18 shows a ceiling effect: CAGP and $U_{\text{str}}$ are \emph{identical} across all subsets (emerging: 0.976, novel: 1.000, overall: 0.987)---coverage fully saturates detection and the semantic signal adds no lift (Appendix~\ref{app:icews18_subsets}). ICEWS18 therefore does not provide independent evidence for complementarity; ICEWS14 emerging entities are the genuine non-circular test. CAGP's primary value on temporal benchmarks is as a \emph{unified} estimator; on ICEWS14 it additionally provides a genuine +2pp emerging-entity gain.

On the static benchmarks, CAGP---the minimal realization of this decomposition---validates these predictions: 0.79--0.91 on emerging entities and 1.0 on novel contexts.\footnote{Our base DistMult \citep{yang2015embedding} achieves MRR/Hits@10 of 0.21/0.34 (FB15k-237) and 0.27/0.42 (WN18RR), comparable to vanilla DistMult in the literature. OOD detection is not sensitive to base model quality (\S\ref{app:architectures}).} Coverage alone achieves 0.67--0.83 on emerging entities; combining with semantic variance adds +8--12pp as predicted. This is consistent with the core message: coverage is strongest where novel-context OOD dominates, but semantic uncertainty is necessary for complete coverage of emerging cases. Overall AUROC (0.90--0.99) reflects the additional boost from novel-context detection. CAGP gaps over the best baseline are consistent across all seeds (mean$\pm$std reported in Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}); with only 3--5 seeds, we do not report p-values, as the minimum achievable bootstrap p-value from $n{=}3$ samples (${\approx}0.125$) does not reach conventional thresholds.

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned scoring does not help.}
We test whether relation-conditioned scoring (bilinear $h^\top W_r t$ with basis decomposition) improves OOD detection when uncertainty remains entity-level.
It does not: novel-context AUROC matches $U_{\text{sem}}$ across all three datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:bilinear}), confirming that relation-aware \emph{scoring} does not produce relation-aware \emph{uncertainty}.
Adding coverage recovers performance to match standard CAGP, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.

\textbf{Theorem predictions vs.\ observations.} Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} checks each part of Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity} on the reported settings:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part (i):} Semantic uncertainty achieves 0.49/0.40/0.40 on novel contexts on static benchmarks (FB15k-237/WN18RR/YAGO). Values below 0.5 on WN18RR and YAGO are expected: novel-context entities are high-frequency by definition, so their learned variances are \emph{lower} than many ID entities (which include rarer entities), making $U_{\text{sem}}$ anti-predictive---a directional consequence of A1. On ICEWS14 and ICEWS18, $U_{\text{sem}}$ achieves 0.78/0.83 on novel contexts---higher because temporal novel contexts are defined by time, not coverage, so they include additional frequency-correlated signal available to entity-level estimators.
\item \textbf{Part (ii):} Structural uncertainty achieves 0.78/0.83/0.67/0.98 on emerging entities (imperfect $<1$ on static benchmarks, as predicted; near-perfect on ICEWS14/18 where most emerging entities also lack coverage).
\item \textbf{Remark~\ref{rem:novel_perfect}:} Structural AUROC $= 1.000$ on novel contexts across the reported settings---by construction on static benchmarks, and also in ICEWS14/18 where the temporal definition is by time and relational novelty.
\item \textbf{Part (iii):} Using the mixture decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}), the predicted CAGP AUROC matches observations to within 0.005 on all datasets (e.g., WN18RR: $0.83 \times 0.91 + 0.17 \times 1.0 \approx 0.93$, observed $0.92$; discrepancy 0.005 due to tie correction; FB15k-237 and YAGO within 0.001). The synergy formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:synergy}) correctly predicts that all improvement comes from emerging entities (+0.08/+0.11/+0.12 over $U_{\text{str}}$ on WN18RR/FB15k-237/YAGO).
\end{itemize}

The complementarity pattern is consistent across all three datasets: on YAGO3-10, CAGP's +12pp gain over $U_{\text{str}}$ on emerging entities is the largest across static benchmarks (see Appendix~\ref{app:discussion} for per-dataset analysis). Across static datasets, semantic uncertainty is weak-to-moderate on novel contexts (0.40--0.49), while structural uncertainty is perfect by construction. CAGP exploits complementarity across all settings (Figure~\ref{fig:complementarity}).

\paragraph{Standard OOD sanity check.}
On standard OOD (random corruptions), CAGP achieves 0.66/0.82 on WN18RR/FB15k-237, competitive with the best single signal, confirming temporal gains do not trade off random-corruption detection. On WN18RR, CAGP is marginally below $U_{\text{sem}}$ (0.67) because random corruptions do not expose entity-relation novelty patterns. Full results and method comparison across OOD types are in Appendix~\ref{app:standard_ood_full}.

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned ablation.}
RelCondVar replaces entity-level variance with $\sigma^2(e,r){=}\text{MLP}([e;r])$ but does not consistently outperform explicit coverage: it achieves 0.980 on FB15k-237 (vs CAGP 0.967, +1.3pp) but matches $U_{\text{str}}$ identically on WN18RR/YAGO (6pp below CAGP). On sparse-relation KGs (WN18RR: 11 relations, YAGO: 37), the MLP has insufficient relation diversity to learn meaningful relation-conditioned variance, so coverage fully dominates and the learned variance contributes zero lift. Without genuine novel-context training examples regardless of relation count, no architecture reliably learns relation-specific variance patterns---this experimentally validates Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}. CAGP remains the default for its parameter-free formulation; practitioners on dense multi-relational KGs may prefer RelCondVar.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
\emph{Temporal OOD.} CAGP's semantic signal lifts WN18RR emerging-entity detection from $U_{\text{str}}$'s 0.83 to 0.91 (overall 0.92 vs 0.86). \emph{Variance collapse.} Omitting reparameterization sampling collapses all entity variances to a uniform value (AUROC drops from 0.92 to 0.72 on WN18RR, 0.90 to 0.68 on YAGO); the reparameterization trick~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding} prevents this. See Appendix~\ref{app:error_analysis} for detailed breakdown and standard OOD error analysis.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\textbf{Static benchmarks validate structure, not independent detection.} Novel-context AUROC $= 1.0$ by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), confirming the decomposition works as designed; these results are diagnostic validation, not performance claims.

\textbf{ICEWS14/18 break this coupling.} CAGP achieves 0.99/0.987 AUROC on ground-truth chronological splits (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}). A leakage audit (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}) confirms temporal integrity; under a strict split (58.5\% removed), CAGP improves to 0.995. Ablations (Appendix~\ref{app:icews14_ablation}) confirm entity-relation structure drives detection: removing coverage drops AUROC to 0.50. ICEWS18 shows a ceiling effect: $U_{\text{str}}$ and CAGP are identical across all subsets (Appendix~\ref{app:icews18_subsets}); CAGP's advantage is concentrated where coverage is imperfect.

See Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity} (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_sensitivity}) for $\alpha$ sensitivity analysis and Appendix~\ref{app:calibration} for calibration analysis.
