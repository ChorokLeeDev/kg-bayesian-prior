\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three static benchmarks: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14,541 entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR} (40,943 entities, 11 relations), and \textbf{YAGO3-10} (123,182 entities, 37 relations), with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits based on entity frequency and co-occurrence (Appendix~\ref{app:ood_splits}). To validate beyond simulated protocols, we also evaluate on \textbf{ICEWS14} (7,128 entities, 230 relations) and \textbf{ICEWS18} (23,033 entities, 256 relations), temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps where train/test splits are chronological---OOD categories emerge naturally from temporal evolution, reducing the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Score-based (UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}), ensemble (MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}), distance-aware (SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}), and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). We omit KG2E~\citep{he2015learning} as its per-relation covariances target link prediction, not OOD detection, and its uncertainty $U = \text{KL}(p(h) \| p(r)) + \text{KL}(p(t) \| p(r))$ remains entity-level (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} applies). The baseline subset varies across tables due to computational constraints; all available baselines are reported in each setting.
All methods share identical OOD splits, evaluation protocol, training hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=$1e-3, 30 epochs), and negative sampling strategy per dataset; ICEWS14/ICEWS18 baselines use the same chronological split and categorization threshold ($\tau{=}$25th percentile).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). We also report calibration diagnostics (ECE, Brier) and selective prediction in the appendix. Statistical significance via paired bootstrap ($p < 0.01$).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All datasets use a unified pipeline with 30 epochs, $\beta_{\text{KL}}{=}0.001$, $w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$, seeds $\{42, 123, 456\}$ (5 seeds for WN18RR; CAGP 5-seed std $= 0.005$, confirming stability). Per-seed breakdowns are in the supplementary material. Code and outputs will be released upon acceptance.
\textbf{Training signal asymmetry:} Models with learned variance (GPOnly, CAGP, RelCondVar) receive an uncertainty margin loss ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$) encouraging higher uncertainty on negative samples; baselines without variance parameters (UKGE, Energy, CoverageOnly) receive only BCE loss. This reflects an architectural constraint, not a selective advantage: the margin loss is undefined for parameter-free uncertainty signals. See Appendix~\ref{app:margin_ablation} for an ablation on the effect of this loss.
Although $\alpha$ is parameterized as a learnable logit, it empirically converges to $\alpha{\approx}0.5$ across all datasets and seeds, consistent with equal weighting being locally optimal under our training objective (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity}).

\textbf{Key findings:} The decomposition framework predicts that combining semantic and structural signals should achieve 0.79--0.91 AUROC on emerging entities and near-perfect detection on novel contexts. ICEWS14 and ICEWS18 are both non-circular validation points: structural coverage is the strongest single-signal contributor (CAGP and $U_{\text{str}}$ are both $\sim$0.99 on overall AUROC). On ICEWS14, the semantic term provides a modest gain on residual emerging entities ($U_{\text{str}}$: 0.975 $\to$ CAGP: 1.000, +2.5pp). On ICEWS18, CAGP and $U_{\text{str}}$ are identical across all subsets (emerging: 0.976, novel contexts: 1.000), indicating coverage fully saturates detection on this benchmark and the semantic signal adds no lift (Appendix~\ref{app:icews18_subsets}). CAGP's primary value on temporal benchmarks is as a \emph{unified} estimator convenient for deployment, not a strict performance improvement over $U_{\text{str}}$ alone. On the static benchmarks, CAGP---the minimal realization of this decomposition---validates these predictions: 0.79--0.91 on emerging entities and 1.0 on novel contexts.\footnote{Our base DistMult \citep{yang2015embedding} achieves MRR/Hits@10 of 0.21/0.34 (FB15k-237) and 0.27/0.42 (WN18RR), comparable to vanilla DistMult in the literature. OOD detection is not sensitive to base model quality (\S\ref{app:architectures}).} Coverage alone achieves 0.67--0.83 on emerging entities; combining with semantic variance adds +8--12pp as predicted. This is consistent with the core message: coverage is strongest where novel-context OOD dominates, but semantic uncertainty is necessary for complete coverage of emerging cases. Overall AUROC (0.90--0.99) reflects the additional boost from novel-context detection. All CAGP vs.\ best-baseline differences are significant ($p < 0.01$, paired bootstrap, 10K iterations).

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned scoring does not help.}
We test whether relation-conditioned scoring (bilinear $h^\top W_r t$ with basis decomposition) improves OOD detection when uncertainty remains entity-level.
It does not: novel-context AUROC matches $U_{\text{sem}}$ across all three datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:bilinear}), confirming that relation-aware \emph{scoring} does not produce relation-aware \emph{uncertainty}.
Adding coverage recovers performance to match standard CAGP, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.

\textbf{Theorem predictions vs.\ observations.} Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} checks each part of Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} on the reported settings:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part (i):} Semantic uncertainty achieves 0.54/0.40/0.40 on novel contexts on static benchmarks (near-random on WN18RR/YAGO; mildly above-random on FB15k-237). On ICEWS14 and ICEWS18, $U_{\text{sem}}$ achieves 0.78/0.83 on novel contexts---higher because temporal novel contexts are defined by time, not coverage, so they include additional frequency-correlated signal
\item \textbf{Part (ii):} Structural uncertainty achieves 0.78/0.83/0.67/0.98 on emerging entities (imperfect $<1$ on static benchmarks, as predicted; near-perfect on ICEWS14/18 where most emerging entities also lack coverage)
\item \textbf{Remark~\ref{rem:novel_perfect}:} Structural AUROC $= 1.000$ on novel contexts across the reported settings---by construction on static benchmarks, and also in ICEWS14/18 where the temporal definition is by time and relation novelty
\item \textbf{Part (iii):} Using the mixture decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}), the predicted CAGP AUROC matches observations to within 0.01 on all datasets (e.g., WN18RR: $0.83 \times 0.91 + 0.17 \times 1.0 \approx 0.93$, observed $0.92$). The synergy formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:synergy}) correctly predicts that all improvement comes from emerging entities (+0.08/+0.11/+0.12 over $U_{\text{str}}$ on static benchmarks)
\end{itemize}

The complementarity pattern is consistent across all three datasets but with different signal strengths: on YAGO3-10, semantic uncertainty on emerging entities is 0.69 while structural is 0.67; on WN18RR (only 11 relations), structural is strong on emerging entities (0.83) while semantic is 0.71; on FB15k-237, semantic is strongest on emerging entities (0.89) while structural is 0.78. Across static datasets, semantic uncertainty remains weak-to-moderate on novel contexts (0.40--0.54), while structural uncertainty is perfect by construction. CAGP's combination exploits this complementarity across all settings (see Figure~\ref{fig:complementarity} for a visual illustration on FB15k-237).

$U_{\text{str}}$ is an explicit deterministic rule that flags OOD when either $(h,r)$ or $(r,t)$ was never observed in training: $2 - c(h,r) - c(t,r)$.

\textbf{YAGO emerging-entity analysis.} On YAGO, CAGP achieves 0.79 on emerging entities, improving over both $U_{\text{sem}}$ (0.69) and $U_{\text{str}}$ (0.67). The mixture decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}) explains the overall gain: CAGP improves emerging detection by +0.10 vs $U_{\text{sem}}$ and +0.12 vs $U_{\text{str}}$, while preserving perfect novel-context detection (1.0), yielding 0.90 overall vs 0.54 for $U_{\text{sem}}$ alone.

\textbf{Why is semantic variance weaker on YAGO?} Both signals are weaker on YAGO than FB15k-237: $U_{\text{sem}}$ achieves 0.69 vs 0.89 on emerging entities, and $U_{\text{str}}$ achieves 0.67 vs 0.78. This is explained by dataset structure: YAGO's variance-frequency correlation (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.68$) is substantially weaker than FB15k-237 ($\rho{=}{-}0.88$), meaning YAGO's entity variances carry less discriminative frequency signal (Assumption A1 more violated; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). Additionally, YAGO's 37 relations---intermediate between WN18RR's 11 and FB15k-237's 237---produce an emerging-entity slice ($\pi_e{=}0.48$) where coverage gaps partially overlap with entity frequency, reducing both signals' separation. Despite this, CAGP's +12pp gain over $U_{\text{str}}$ on YAGO is the largest across all static benchmarks, showing the combination is most valuable precisely where single signals are weakest.

\paragraph{Relation-conditioned variance ablation.}
We also evaluated RelCondVar, which replaces entity-level variance with a learned $\sigma^2(e,r) = \text{MLP}([e; r])$ while retaining coverage ($\alpha{=}0.5$, fixed). We treat this as a fairness/control check on training signal: relation-conditioned variance alone is not a strict replacement for explicit structural coverage. On FB15k-237, using reparameterization-aware scoring raises RelCondVar temporal AUROC to 0.980 (emerging 0.933, std$<0.002$), still not consistently above explicit coverage-driven variants. CAGP remains the default method due to its parameter-free formulation and stable behavior across datasets.

\paragraph{Why does the MLP learn nothing?}
Three factors conspire: (1)~RelCondVar uses reparameterization-aware scoring in this setup, but random-corruption OOD examples still provide little relation-specific signal about emerging contexts; (2)~random corruptions do not expose the non-coverage relation novelty patterns that relation-conditioned variance needs to learn; and (3)~the coverage component already nearly perfectly separates novel contexts in the margin loss, leaving limited residual signal for the MLP to capture. This is not a capacity limitation---it is a \emph{training-signal} limitation: without examples of genuine novel contexts during training, no architecture can reliably learn relation-specific variance patterns from standard objectives. This experimentally validates Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}.

\paragraph{Empirical vs theoretical AUROC.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity} predicts semantic AUROC $\approx 0.5$ on novel contexts, while Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} shows 0.40--0.54. This is consistent with Assumption A3 being approximate: frequency matching between novel-context and ID subsets is imperfect, so $U_{\text{sem}}$ can drift slightly below or above 0.5 depending on dataset composition. The Spearman-based bound (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}) remains consistent with these deviations.

\paragraph{Binary vs continuous coverage.}
To test whether co-occurrence frequency improves upon binary presence/absence, we evaluated continuous coverage formulations (log-scaled, TF-IDF) on temporal OOD. Binary coverage achieves AUROC $= 1.0$ on FB15k-237 novel contexts (expected by construction; see Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}), while continuous variants (log-scaled, TF-IDF) achieve only 0.56--0.59 (near-random). The key insight is that continuous coverage introduces frequency-based variations among observed pairs that obscure the binary observed/unobserved boundary, causing ID and OOD uncertainty distributions to overlap (Table~\ref{tab:coverage_ablation}). For emerging entities, GP variance already captures frequency through learned embeddings, making explicit frequency counts redundant.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
\emph{Temporal OOD.} On WN18RR, $U_{\text{str}}$ alone achieves 0.86 AUROC; the gap from 1.0 reflects emerging entities where coverage is non-zero ($\rho = 0.34$; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). CAGP closes this gap: its semantic signal lifts emerging-entity detection from 0.83 to 0.91, yielding 0.92 overall.

\emph{Variance collapse without sampling.} A na\"ive implementation that omits reparameterization sampling in the scoring function suffers \emph{training collapse}: KL regularization drives all entity variances to a uniform value ($\sigma^2_e \to 1.0$, std${=}0$ across entities) since the scoring function does not use the variance parameters and provides no counter-gradient. Train-set calibration then yields $\mu_{\text{cov}}{=}0$ (all training triples are covered by construction), scaling the degenerate GP signal by ${\sim}10^{-8}$---the combined uncertainty reduces to float noise for covered triples. This collapse reduces AUROC from 0.92 to 0.72 on WN18RR and from 0.90 to 0.68 on YAGO. The fix is standard variational inference: using the reparameterization trick \citep{kingma2014autoencoding} in the scoring function ensures that the reconstruction loss (BCE) provides gradients to the variance parameters, preventing KL-driven collapse. This diagnosis applies to any probabilistic KG embedding that learns per-entity variances.

Matched-coverage analysis further confirms the semantic signal's value: among test triples sharing the same coverage value, GP semantic uncertainty alone separates emerging from ID with 0.79 AUROC (WN18RR) and 0.86 AUROC (FB15k-237).

\emph{Standard OOD.} An additional error analysis on FB15k-237 using prediction-score-based detection (20K balanced samples) yields 0.971 AUROC---higher than Table~\ref{tab:standard} because prediction scores are strong corruption detectors.
False positives (8.7\%) occur on triples with low-degree tails; false negatives (8.7\%) involve corrupted tails that coincidentally create higher-degree entities.
See Appendix~\ref{app:error_analysis} for detailed breakdown.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\textbf{The static benchmarks validate decomposition structure, not independent detection capability.} On the three static benchmarks, the novel-context category is defined using the same coverage indicator as our structural detector, so structural AUROC $= 1$ on this subset is guaranteed by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}). This confirms the decomposition works as designed---per-category AUROC matches theoretical predictions within 0.002---but means the static results provide diagnostic validation, not performance claims.

\textbf{ICEWS14 and ICEWS18 break this coupling.} With ground-truth chronological splits, CAGP achieves 0.99 AUROC on ICEWS14 and 0.987 AUROC on ICEWS18 (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}, and section text). A leakage audit (Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}) confirms temporal integrity for ICEWS14; under a strict split removing all duplicates and inverse overlaps (58.5\% removed), CAGP improves to 0.995. Ablations (Appendix~\ref{app:icews14_ablation}) confirm the specific entity-relation structure drives detection: removing coverage ($\alpha{=}1$) drops AUROC to 0.50; shuffling entity-relation associations drops to 0.55; random coverage at matched density drops to 0.50. On ICEWS14, coverage dominates ($>$96\% of temporal OOD has $U_{\text{str}} > 0$), so CAGP matches $U_{\text{str}}$ overall; the semantic signal's contribution is concentrated on the emerging-entity slice (0.975$\to$1.000), consistent with the mixture decomposition where $\pi_e{=}0.30$ limits visible synergy. On ICEWS18, $U_{\text{str}}$ and CAGP are identical across all subsets (Appendix~\ref{app:icews18_subsets}): coverage alone achieves ceiling performance and the semantic signal adds no gain. We acknowledge this ceiling effect honestly: CAGP's advantage on temporal benchmarks is most visible where coverage is imperfect (ICEWS14 emerging, and the static benchmarks where $\pi_e$ is larger). On fully coverage-saturated benchmarks, CAGP reduces to a convenient single-score equivalent of $U_{\text{str}}$.

\textbf{Additional supporting evidence.} (1)~Semantic uncertainty on novel contexts is limited (0.40--0.54; Table~\ref{tab:complementarity}), while structural coverage is perfect on this subset by construction. (2)~Continuous coverage variants achieve only 0.56--0.59 (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage_ablation}), confirming binary presence/absence is the discriminative signal. (3)~Matched-coverage analysis: among test triples sharing the same coverage value, GP semantic uncertainty alone separates emerging from ID with 0.79 AUROC (WN18RR) and 0.86 AUROC (FB15k-237). (4)~RelCondVar does not provide consistent gains over $U_{\text{str}}$, while CAGP remains strongest overall.

See Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity} (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_sensitivity}) for $\alpha$ sensitivity analysis and Appendix~\ref{app:calibration} for calibration analysis.
