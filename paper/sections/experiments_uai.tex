\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
Three static benchmarks with \emph{simulated temporal OOD} splits: \textbf{FB15k-237} (14.5K entities, 237 relations), \textbf{WN18RR} (40.9K, 11), \textbf{YAGO3-10} (123K, 37). Two temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps: \textbf{ICEWS14} (7.1K, 230) and \textbf{ICEWS18} (23K, 256), where OOD categories emerge from chronological splits---breaking the definitional coupling between coverage and OOD labels.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding}, Energy~\citep{liu2020energy}, MC Dropout~\citep{gal2016dropout}, Deep Ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, SNGP~\citep{liu2020simple}, and single signals ($U_{\text{sem}}$, $U_{\text{str}}$). All share identical splits, hyperparameters (dim$=$100, lr$=10^{-3}$, 30 epochs), and evaluation protocol. Standalone $U_{\text{sem}}$ (GPOnly) reports raw entity variance; the CAGP-internal semantic component is rescaled (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Metrics and reproducibility.}
AUROC (primary; AUPR in Appendix~\ref{app:aupr}). Base seeds $\{42, 123, 456\}$; extended to 5 seeds for WN18RR and ICEWS14, 10 for FB15k-237 (Appendix~\ref{app:per_seed}). Per-seed breakdowns show standard deviations $\leq 0.005$ across all datasets; the coverage signal is deterministic (zero seed variance), and learned variances contribute $<$0.01 AUROC variation. Models with learned variance receive $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0.1$); ablation in \S\ref{sec:ablations} confirms that $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ contributes $<$0.4pp. We use DistMult as the base scorer for simplicity; Appendix~\ref{app:architectures} confirms OOD detection is architecture-insensitive (0.959--0.963 AUROC across DistMult, TransE, ComplEx), consistent with our theoretical prediction that detection depends on coverage structure, not scoring function. Base DistMult achieves MRR/Hits@10 of 0.21/0.34 (FB15k-237) and 0.27/0.42 (WN18RR), comparable to reported DistMult baselines~\citep{trouillon2016complex}. We do not compare Mahalanobis distance (the OOD signal is relational, not geometric), conformal prediction (targets prediction-set guarantees, not detection), or evidential methods (targets classification, not KG triple scoring).

\textbf{Key findings} (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}).

\emph{Non-circular evidence (ICEWS14/18).} On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps, coverage dominates: $U_{\text{str}}$ achieves 0.99 AUROC on both ICEWS benchmarks, and CAGP matches this. The semantic component adds +2pp on emerging entities for ICEWS14 (1.00 vs 0.98); on ICEWS18, CAGP performs identically to $U_{\text{str}}$ across all OOD categories due to a ceiling effect. These results confirm that \emph{structural uncertainty is the primary missing ingredient}---the gap between baselines (0.38--0.87) and coverage (0.99) is the core finding.

\emph{Diagnostic evidence (static benchmarks).} On simulated OOD splits, semantic variance adds +8--12pp on emerging entities over coverage alone. The mixture-AUROC decomposition (Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture_auroc}) serves as a \emph{consistency check}: predicted and observed AUROC match within 0.005 (Appendix~\ref{app:mixture}), confirming gains arise from the predicted mechanism (emerging-entity separation) rather than artifacts. Novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}); these results test the decomposition's \emph{internal structure}, not independent detection capability.

\emph{RelCondVar.} Outperforms CAGP on FB15k-237 (+1pp overall, +4pp emerging) but reduces to $U_{\text{str}}$ on sparse-relation KGs (WN18RR/YAGO), where insufficient relation diversity prevents learning meaningful conditioned variance (\S\ref{sec:relcondvar}).

\emph{Why does semantic help on static but not temporal?} On ICEWS14/18, nearly all emerging entities have zero coverage for the test relation (68\% of ICEWS14 emerging entities are entirely absent from training; Appendix~\ref{app:leakage}), so $U_{\text{str}}$ already captures them---leaving no room for semantic tiebreaking. On static benchmarks, 34--66\% of emerging entities \emph{do} have coverage for the query relation ($\rho$ in Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}), creating ties that semantic uncertainty breaks. The Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}(iii) formula predicts exactly this: the gain is $\pi_e \cdot (A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{comb}} - A_{\text{emerge}}^{\text{str}})$, which is positive only when emerging entities have non-trivial coverage overlap ($\rho > 0$).

\subsection{Validating Complementarity}

Table~\ref{tab:complementarity} confirms each part of Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}: $U_{\text{sem}}$ achieves near-random AUROC on novel contexts (0.40--0.49 on static benchmarks; anti-predictive due to high-frequency novel-context entities having lower variance than the in-distribution average). $U_{\text{str}}$ is imperfect on emerging entities (0.67--0.83 on static; near-perfect on ICEWS where most emerging entities lack coverage). The combination helps: CAGP's gain comes entirely from emerging entities (+8--12pp over $U_{\text{str}}$), matching the mixture decomposition to within 0.005 (Appendix~\ref{app:mixture}):
\begin{equation}
\text{AUROC}(U) = \pi_e \cdot A_{\text{emerge}}(U) + \pi_n \cdot A_{\text{novel}}(U) + \delta_{\text{tie}}
\label{eq:mixture_auroc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adversarial Evaluation: ICEWS14 Strict Split}
\label{sec:strict_split}

Inverse relations (\emph{visited} in train, \emph{was\_visited\_by} in test) can inflate temporal OOD performance. We construct a \emph{strict split} removing all inverse-relation overlap (58.5\% of test triples removed).

Energy collapses to chance (0.54$\to$0.50); $U_{\text{sem}}$ degrades (0.82$\to$0.79), confirming that relation-agnostic variance provides no reliable signal for temporal OOD even on temporal data---consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}. \subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Margin loss.}
Removing $\mathcal{L}_{\text{margin}}$ ($w_{\text{unc}}{=}0$) causes $<$0.4pp degradation on WN18RR (0.669$\to$0.666), confirming CAGP's gains derive from the coverage-semantic decomposition, not the auxiliary training objective.

\paragraph{Baseline + coverage.}
Post-hoc coverage augmentation ($0.5 \cdot U_{\text{baseline}} + 0.5 \cdot U_{\text{str}}$) converges to coverage-only performance without matching CAGP: Energy+Cov$=$0.845, MC~Dropout+Cov$=$0.859, CoverageOnly$=$0.859, vs.\ CAGP$=$0.92 on WN18RR. The +6pp gap arises because baseline variances lack meaningful frequency-differentiation without reparameterization sampling (cf.\ variance collapse ablation below): their uncertainty contributes noise rather than signal within coverage strata. CAGP's reparameterization-trained variances produce a rank-preserving tiebreaker that separates emerging entities \emph{within} the $U_{\text{str}}{=}0$ stratum (where 34--66\% of emerging entities fall; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).

\paragraph{Variance collapse.}
Omitting reparameterization sampling collapses entity variances (AUROC: 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR, 0.90$\to$0.68 on YAGO). The reparameterization trick is essential.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
Static novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction; these are diagnostic, not performance claims. ICEWS14 strict split breaks this coupling. ICEWS18 shows a ceiling: $U_{\text{str}}=$ CAGP across all subsets. Additional temporal benchmarks (e.g., GDELT) are needed to further validate complementarity.

\paragraph{Standard OOD.}
On standard random-corruption OOD (Appendix~\ref{app:standard_ood_full}), CAGP ties the best single signal (0.82 on FB15k-237) and is marginally below $U_{\text{sem}}$ on WN18RR (0.66 vs 0.67): coverage provides no information when corruptions sample entities that already have the query relation in training. CAGP is designed for temporal OOD; standard OOD is a complementary setting where entity-level methods are sufficient.
