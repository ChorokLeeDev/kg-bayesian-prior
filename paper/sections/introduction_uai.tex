\paragraph{A Systematic Blind Spot.}
Every major probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) embedding method---variational encoders~\citep{chen2019embedding,chen2021probabilistic}, Bayesian approaches~\citep{choudhary2021perm}, ensemble methods, energy scoring---shares a common failure mode. When a well-observed entity appears in a \emph{novel relational context} (a relation it was never trained with), these methods report low uncertainty despite having no evidence. \textbf{Among Energy scoring's 100 most confident predictions on FB15k-237, 83\% have zero training evidence}---2.6$\times$ higher than the 32\% base rate (Table~\ref{tab:confident_wrong}). Under strict evaluation removing inverse-relation leakage, Energy collapses from 0.59 to chance (0.50), while a simple coverage check maintains 1.00 AUROC (Table~\ref{tab:strict_split}).

\paragraph{Prevalence: 11--25\% of Queries.}
This is not a rare edge case. Across five benchmarks, 11--25\% of test triples involve novel relational contexts: familiar entities appearing in relationships never observed during training. These queries receive confidently wrong uncertainty estimates from all existing methods. The failure has remained hidden because (1) standard OOD benchmarks use random splits that leak inverse relations, (2) evaluation reports aggregate AUROC that masks per-category failures, and (3) no prior work has separately measured novel-context detection.

\paragraph{Why Does This Happen?}
The core intuition is straightforward: entity $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ receives low variance (well-observed), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ are genuinely uncertain. Any uncertainty of the form $f(\sigma^2_h, \sigma^2_t)$---where variance depends only on entity identity---cannot distinguish these cases. We formalize this intuition in Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}, which characterizes precisely when relation-agnostic methods fail and establishes the frequency-matching conditions under which AUROC degrades to chance. The theorem is not a surprising impossibility---rather, it makes rigorous \emph{why} existing methods fail, connecting the failure to the information-theoretic structure of KG embeddings. More generally, any uncertainty derived from standard KG embeddings inherits this blindness (Theorem~\ref{thm:embedding_impossibility}): embeddings are trained to score triples correctly, but do not encode \emph{which relations} each entity has seen.

\paragraph{The Fix is Trivial.}
Track which (entity, relation) pairs appeared during training via a hash table. This $O(1)$ lookup with ${<}2\%$ overhead catches the failure mode invisible to all learned uncertainty methods. The diagnosis is the contribution---the fix was always available but never adopted because the problem was never quantified.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This paper is primarily a \emph{diagnostic critique} of existing KG uncertainty evaluation, not a methods paper.
\textbf{(1) Problem quantification}: We measure a systematic blind spot affecting 11--25\% of KG queries, previously hidden by aggregate metrics and leaky evaluation protocols. The 83\% confident-wrong rate demonstrates that existing methods are not merely uncertain---they are \emph{systematically overconfident} on zero-evidence queries.
\textbf{(2) Theoretical characterization}: We formalize why relation-agnostic uncertainty estimators fail on novel contexts (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}), making rigorous an intuition that---while perhaps unsurprising in retrospect---has not prevented the field from deploying these methods without coverage tracking.
\textbf{(3) Empirical validation}: On ICEWS14/18/GDELT with ground-truth temporal splits, coverage alone achieves 0.99--1.00 AUROC while baselines collapse to chance. Crucially, on GDELT with high coverage overlap ($\rho = 0.84$), semantic still achieves random AUROC---confirming that the limitation holds regardless of $\rho$. On static benchmarks where emerging entities have partial coverage, semantic adds +8--11pp.
\textbf{(4) Practical recommendation}: Always track entity-relation coverage. This zero-cost fix should be standard practice for any deployed KG system.
\textbf{(5) Proof-of-concept}: CAGP demonstrates that combining structural and semantic signals achieves 0.90--0.97 AUROC; the method itself is deliberately simple because the decomposition---not the architecture---is the contribution.
