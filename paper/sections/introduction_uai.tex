% Introduction for UAI - Uncertainty focus

Knowledge graph (KG) embeddings map entities and relations to continuous vector spaces, enabling efficient reasoning over large-scale structured knowledge~\citep{bordes2013translating,trouillon2016complex,sun2019rotate}.
These embeddings underlie applications from question answering~\citep{huang2019knowledge} to drug discovery~\citep{mohamed2020discovering}.
As KGs evolve---new entities emerge, existing entities participate in novel relationships---robust uncertainty quantification becomes critical for reliable deployment.

\paragraph{Probabilistic Embeddings.}
Recent work addresses uncertainty by learning distributions over embeddings rather than point estimates.
GP-KGE~\citep{Chen2021PERM} places Gaussian distributions over entity representations; BEUrRE~\citep{chen2021probabilistic} uses box embeddings where box size indicates uncertainty; UKGE~\citep{chen2019embedding} associates confidence scores with triples.
The intuition is sound: rare entities, observed in few training triples, should have higher variance reflecting epistemic uncertainty about their true representations.

\paragraph{A Fundamental Limitation.}
These methods share a critical blind spot: learned variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}.
An entity $e$ receives a single variance $\sigma^2_e$ regardless of relational context.
Consider entity $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ but never with $r_{11}$.
The model assigns low variance (well-observed entity), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ involve genuinely uncertain predictions---the entity-relation combination was never observed.

This limitation has measurable consequences.
Score-based uncertainty methods (using prediction confidence) achieve 0.99 AUROC detecting random corruptions---but only 0.52--0.58 on temporally-shifted queries where OOD samples are structurally similar to training data.
The methods optimize for the wrong distribution shift.

\paragraph{Two Types of OOD.}
We identify two distinct OOD scenarios requiring different detection strategies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Emerging entities}: New entities (or rarely-observed ones) with poorly-constrained embeddings. High entity variance correctly signals uncertainty.
    \item \textbf{Novel relational contexts}: Well-observed entities in unobserved relational contexts. Entity variance is low (falsely confident), but the specific entity-relation pair was never trained.
\end{enumerate}

Under temporal distribution shift---the realistic scenario where KGs evolve---both types occur.
Conflating them causes systematic failures.

\paragraph{Our Contribution.}
We propose decomposing KG uncertainty into semantic and structural components:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Semantic uncertainty} $U_{\text{sem}}$: Derived from entity embedding variance. Captures how well each entity is learned.
    \item \textbf{Structural uncertainty} $U_{\text{str}}$: Derived from entity-relation co-occurrence (coverage). Captures whether a specific relational context was observed.
\end{itemize}

Our main theoretical result (Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity}) proves these signals are complementary:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Semantic uncertainty cannot detect novel contexts (achieves $\leq 0.5 + \epsilon$ AUROC)
    \item Structural uncertainty cannot reliably detect all emerging entities (achieves $< 1$ AUROC)
    \item Any convex combination strictly dominates either signal alone when both OOD types are present
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Empirical Validation.}
We validate on four benchmarks: FB15k-237, WN18RR, YAGO3-10, and ICEWS14 (with ground-truth temporal splits).
Our decomposition achieves 0.87--0.97 AUROC on temporal OOD, compared to 0.52--0.58 for existing uncertainty methods.
On selective prediction, abstaining on 15\% of queries with highest uncertainty reduces errors by 48\%.

The framework is architecture-agnostic (improving DistMult, TransE, ComplEx uniformly) and computationally lightweight (coverage is precomputed once from training data).
