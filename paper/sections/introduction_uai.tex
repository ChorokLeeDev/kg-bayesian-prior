% Introduction for UAI - condensed

Knowledge graph (KG) embeddings enable efficient reasoning over structured knowledge~\citep{bordes2013translating,trouillon2016complex}.
As KGs evolve---new entities emerge, existing entities appear in novel relationships---robust uncertainty quantification becomes critical.

\paragraph{The Relation-Agnostic Limitation.}
Recent probabilistic methods learn distributions over embeddings~\citep{chen2019embedding,chen2021probabilistic,Chen2021PERM}, where rare entities have higher variance.
But these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}: entity $e$ receives a single variance $\sigma^2_e$ regardless of relational context.
Consider $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ but never with $r_{11}$.
The model assigns low variance (well-observed), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ involve genuinely uncertain predictions.

This causes systematic failures (Figure~\ref{fig:main_results}): existing probabilistic methods achieve only 0.38--0.80 AUROC on temporal OOD (emerging entities and novel relational contexts), while our decomposition-based approach reaches 0.90--0.99.

\paragraph{Two OOD Types.}
We identify two distinct scenarios:
(1) \textbf{Emerging entities}: New/rare entities with poorly-constrained embeddings---high variance correctly signals uncertainty.
(2) \textbf{Novel contexts}: Well-observed entities in unobserved relations---variance is low (falsely confident), but the entity-relation pair was never trained.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We diagnose why probabilistic KG embeddings fail at temporal OOD detection and show how to fix them.

\textbf{(1) Impossibility result:} We prove that \emph{any} relation-agnostic uncertainty estimator---one whose output depends only on entity-level statistics---achieves near-random detection on novel relational contexts (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). This directly explains the poor performance of UKGE, Energy, MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles, and SNGP (0.38--0.80 AUROC) on temporal distribution shift.
\textbf{(2) Decomposition with mixture AUROC:} We decompose KG uncertainty into \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (binary entity-relation co-occurrence) components and prove their complementary failure modes (Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity}): semantic uncertainty detects emerging entities but is blind to novel contexts; structural uncertainty detects novel contexts perfectly but degrades on emerging entities. An exact mixture AUROC decomposition predicts overall performance to within 0.002 on all datasets.
\textbf{(3) CAGP realizes the complementarity:} Our model combines semantic and structural signals, achieving 0.90--0.97 AUROC across WN18RR~\citep{dettmers2018convolutional}, FB15k-237~\citep{toutanova2015observed}, and YAGO3-10~\citep{mahdisoltani2015yago3}---consistently outperforming coverage alone (0.84--0.94). The gain is concentrated on emerging entities (0.79--0.91 AUROC, +8--12pp over coverage), precisely where the theory predicts synergy. On ICEWS14 (ground-truth temporal), CAGP achieves 0.993 AUROC.
\textbf{(4) Variance collapse diagnosis:} We identify a subtle failure mode in variational KG embeddings: when the scoring function ignores variance parameters, KL regularization collapses all entity variances to a uniform value. Reparameterization sampling in the scoring function prevents this collapse, enabling meaningful semantic uncertainty. This diagnosis applies broadly to probabilistic KG embedding methods.
