Knowledge graph (KG) embeddings enable efficient reasoning over structured knowledge~\citep{bordes2013translating,trouillon2016complex}.
As KGs evolve---new entities emerge, existing entities appear in novel relationships---robust uncertainty quantification becomes critical.

\paragraph{The Relation-Agnostic Limitation.}
Recent probabilistic methods learn distributions over embeddings~\citep{chen2019embedding,chen2021probabilistic,choudhary2021perm}, where rare entities have higher variance.
But these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}: entity $e$ receives a single variance $\sigma^2_e$ regardless of relational context.
Consider $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ but never with $r_{11}$.
The model assigns low variance (well-observed), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ involve genuinely uncertain predictions.

This causes systematic failures: on temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14), score-based methods achieve 0.38--0.59 AUROC and ensemble methods reach at most 0.80; under a strict protocol removing inverse-relation overlap, Energy collapses from 0.59 to chance (0.50), while structural coverage maintains 1.00 AUROC (Table~\ref{tab:strict_split}). The gap between these numbers---not improved by ensembles, dropout, or energy scoring---is the core phenomenon our impossibility theorem explains for variance-based methods and that we empirically document for the broader class.

\paragraph{Two OOD Types.}
We identify two distinct scenarios:
(1) \textbf{Emerging entities}: New/rare entities with poorly-constrained embeddings---high variance correctly signals uncertainty.
(2) \textbf{Novel contexts}: Well-observed entities in unobserved relations---variance is low (falsely confident), but the entity-relation pair was never trained.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We diagnose why probabilistic KG embeddings fail at temporal OOD detection and provide a theoretically grounded fix.

\textbf{(1) Impossibility result (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}):} Any uncertainty estimator of the form $f(\sigma^2_h, \sigma^2_t)$---where $\sigma^2_e$ depends only on entity $e$---achieves near-random AUROC on novel relational contexts. This formally explains the failure of variance-based methods (GP-KGE); score-based and ensemble methods (UKGE, Energy, MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles, SNGP) do not satisfy the theorem's formal preconditions but exhibit consistently weak temporal-OOD performance, compatible with the impossibility argument.

\textbf{(2) Decomposition with mixture AUROC (Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}):} KG uncertainty decomposes into \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation coverage) components with complementary, predictable failure modes. An exact mixture-AUROC identity predicts overall detection within 0.005 on all static benchmarks, providing a quantitative test of the theory.

\textbf{(3) Empirical validation:} We construct CAGP---a minimal weighted combination of both signals---to validate the decomposition. On ICEWS14 under a strict temporal split removing 41.2\% inverse-relation leakage, coverage alone achieves 1.00 AUROC while Energy collapses to chance (0.50) and entity-variance degrades (0.78 $\to$ 0.69). CAGP matches coverage on ICEWS14/18, confirming structural uncertainty as the primary missing ingredient; on static benchmarks, the semantic signal adds +8--11pp on emerging entities (diagnostic complementarity validation; novel-context AUROC$=$1.0 by construction, Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}).

\textbf{(4) Ablation evidence:} Post-hoc coverage augmentation of baselines (Energy+Coverage, MC~Dropout+Coverage) does not match CAGP, showing that the trained semantic component provides genuine tiebreaking value. Removing the margin loss causes $<$0.1pp degradation, ruling out training signal asymmetry.
