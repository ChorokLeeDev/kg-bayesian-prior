\paragraph{An Empirical Discovery.}
Among Energy scoring's 100 most confident predictions on FB15k-237, \textbf{78\%$\pm$3\% have zero training evidence}---the model has never seen these entity-relation pairs, yet assigns them highest confidence. This is 2.4$\times$ higher than the 32\% base rate (Table~\ref{tab:confident_wrong}). The pattern holds across methods: UKGE shows 80\%$\pm$4\%, and both methods exhibit 1.5--2.1$\times$ elevation even after controlling for entity frequency. This is not miscalibration in the usual sense---it is \emph{systematic overconfidence on zero-evidence queries}.

\paragraph{The Failure is Pervasive.}
Across seven benchmarks, 11--25\% of test queries involve \emph{novel relational contexts}: familiar entities appearing in relationships never observed during training. Every major uncertainty method---variational embeddings~\citep{chen2019embedding,chen2021probabilistic}, Bayesian approaches~\citep{choudhary2021perm}, ensembles, energy scoring, and even foundation models like ULTRA~\citep{galkin2024ultra}---fails identically on these queries (AUROC $\leq$ 0.50). The failure has remained hidden because standard evaluation aggregates over query types, masking category-specific collapse.

\paragraph{Why This Happens.}
The explanation is intuitive: entity $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ receives low variance (well-observed), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ are genuinely uncertain. Any uncertainty depending only on entity identity cannot distinguish these cases. We formalize this in Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}: under empirically-verified conditions, relation-agnostic uncertainty achieves AUROC $\leq 0.5$ on novel contexts. The theorem explains the 78\% finding---high-frequency entities receive low uncertainty regardless of which relation is queried.

\paragraph{The Fix.}
Track which (entity, relation) pairs appeared during training via a hash table. This $O(1)$ lookup catches the failure mode invisible to all learned methods. On ICEWS14/18 with ground-truth temporal splits, coverage achieves 0.99 AUROC while Energy collapses to chance (Table~\ref{tab:strict_split}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1) Empirical discovery}: We identify systematic overconfidence on zero-evidence queries---78\% of top-confident predictions lack any training evidence. This finding, not previously reported, reveals a fundamental evaluation gap.
\textbf{(2) Prevalence quantification}: 11--25\% of test queries across seven benchmarks fall into this blind spot, making it a substantial rather than edge-case failure.
\textbf{(3) Theoretical explanation}: Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} formalizes why the failure occurs, providing testable conditions (A1--A3) that predict when AUROC degrades to chance.
\textbf{(4) Validation}: On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14/18), coverage achieves 0.99 AUROC---confirming the diagnosis is correct and the fix works.
\textbf{(5) Practical recommendation}: Always track entity-relation coverage. For billion-entity KGs, Bloom filters reduce storage 5$\times$ with $<$0.2pp recall loss.
