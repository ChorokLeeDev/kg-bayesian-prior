% Introduction for UAI - condensed

Knowledge graph (KG) embeddings enable efficient reasoning over structured knowledge~\citep{bordes2013translating,trouillon2016complex}.
As KGs evolve---new entities emerge, existing entities appear in novel relationships---robust uncertainty quantification becomes critical.

\paragraph{The Relation-Agnostic Limitation.}
Recent probabilistic methods learn distributions over embeddings~\citep{chen2019embedding,chen2021probabilistic,choudhary2021perm}, where rare entities have higher variance.
But these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}: entity $e$ receives a single variance $\sigma^2_e$ regardless of relational context.
Consider $e$ appearing in 1000 triples with relations $\{r_1, \ldots, r_{10}\}$ but never with $r_{11}$.
The model assigns low variance (well-observed), yet queries $(?, r_{11}, e)$ involve genuinely uncertain predictions.

This causes systematic failures (Figure~\ref{fig:main_results}): probabilistic methods achieve only 0.34--0.87 AUROC on temporal OOD, while our decomposition-based approach reaches 0.90--0.99. On the non-circular ground-truth benchmarks (ICEWS14 and ICEWS18, where OOD is defined by time rather than coverage), CAGP achieves 0.993 and 0.987 AUROC respectively.

\paragraph{Two OOD Types.}
We identify two distinct scenarios:
(1) \textbf{Emerging entities}: New/rare entities with poorly-constrained embeddings---high variance correctly signals uncertainty.
(2) \textbf{Novel contexts}: Well-observed entities in unobserved relations---variance is low (falsely confident), but the entity-relation pair was never trained.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We diagnose why probabilistic KG embeddings fail at temporal OOD detection and show how to fix them.

\textbf{(1) Impossibility result:} We prove that any relation-agnostic uncertainty estimator---whose output uses only entity-level statistics---is near-random on novel relational contexts (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). This formally explains variance-based methods (GP-KGE); for score-based and ensemble methods (UKGE, Energy, MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles, SNGP), the theorem's preconditions are not met, but empirical performance is near-random on novel contexts (0.34--0.87 AUROC across all benchmarks), consistent with the impossibility argument.

\textbf{(2) Decomposition with mixture AUROC:} We split KG uncertainty into \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components, with complementary failure modes (Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}): semantic uncertainty is effective on emerging entities but weak on novel contexts; structural uncertainty is strong on novel contexts but imperfect on emerging entities. An exact mixture AUROC decomposition predicts overall results within 0.005 on all static benchmarks (within 0.001 on FB15k-237 and YAGO; the WN18RR discrepancy of 0.005 arises from tie correction).

\textbf{(3) Minimal realization:} We construct CAGP---combining normalized semantic and binary coverage signals---to validate the decomposition framework. Empirically, CAGP reaches 0.99 AUROC on ICEWS14/18 (ground-truth temporal splits, no circularity) and 0.90--0.97 on simulated-OOD benchmarks, with emerging-entity gains of +8--12pp over coverage alone; overall AUROC matches the mixture formula throughout.

\textbf{(4) Variance collapse diagnostic:} We identify that when the scoring function ignores learned variance parameters, KL regularization collapses entity variances to a uniform value, erasing the semantic signal; reparameterization sampling prevents this collapse. This pitfall applies to any variational KGE where variance parameters are excluded from the forward pass during training.
