Probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings use learned variances to quantify epistemic uncertainty, but these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}---an entity receives identical uncertainty regardless of relational context. This conflates two distinct OOD phenomena: \emph{emerging entities} (rare, poorly-learned) and \emph{novel relational contexts} (familiar entities in unobserved relationships). We prove that, under empirically validated frequency-matching assumptions, any uncertainty estimator using only entity-level statistics achieves near-random detection on novel contexts, explaining why existing methods score 0.99 AUROC on random corruptions but only 0.44--0.64 on temporal distribution shift. We decompose uncertainty into complementary \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components, and show that neither alone suffices. Our method CAGP combines both signals via learned weights, achieving 0.94--1.00 AUROC on temporal OOD across three benchmarks, substantially improving over relation-agnostic baselines.
