% Abstract for UAI - Diagnosis + working CAGP

Probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings assign each entity a single variance regardless of relational context. We prove this is a fundamental limitation: under empirically validated assumptions, any relation-agnostic uncertainty estimator achieves near-random detection of novel relational contexts---familiar entities queried in unobserved relationships. This impossibility result directly explains why existing methods (UKGE, Energy, MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles, SNGP) achieve only 0.38--0.80 AUROC on temporal distribution shift. We decompose KG uncertainty into \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components and prove their complementary failure modes: under our fixed tie policy, the overall AUROC decomposes approximately as $\pi_e A_{\text{emerge}} + \pi_n A_{\text{novel}} + \delta_{\text{tie}}$, with each signal dominating on one sub-population. To validate this framework, we construct a minimal realization (CAGP) that combines both signals. On ICEWS14 with ground-truth temporal splits---the only non-circular test of temporal OOD---CAGP achieves 0.993 AUROC. On static benchmarks, the decomposition predicts per-category performance within 0.002, with 0.79--0.91 AUROC on emerging entities (the non-trivial detection task where semantic uncertainty contributes). Realizing this complementarity requires reparameterization sampling to prevent variance collapse under KL regularization.
