% Abstract - NeurIPS revision (Blind Spot Exposure framing)

Among Energy scoring's 100 most confident predictions on knowledge graphs (KGs), 78\%$\pm$3\% have zero training evidence---the model is most confident precisely where it has never seen data. This systematic blind spot affects every major uncertainty method (variational embeddings, ensembles, energy scoring) and 11--25\% of test queries across seven benchmarks.

We explain why: embeddings encode coverage information (AUC$=$0.96$\pm$0.04 for predicting entity-relation co-occurrence within frequency strata), but standard uncertainty methods cannot extract it. Even an MLP with explicit OOD supervision achieves 0.50 AUROC---exactly random. The information exists but is architecturally inaccessible to variance, energy, and ensemble methods. This explains why more training, larger models, and foundation architectures (ULTRA) all fail identically.

On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14/18), a simple coverage check achieves 0.99 AUROC while all baselines collapse to chance. On GDELT with high coverage overlap ($\rho = 0.84$), coverage fails too (0.57 AUROC), revealing the method's boundary. The fix is trivial: track entity-relation coverage via hash table ($O(1)$ lookup). The contribution is the diagnosis---quantifying and explaining a pervasive failure mode that standard evaluation has masked. The same blind spot likely affects other structured prediction domains where feature co-occurrence matters: recommender systems (user-item coverage), drug-target interaction (compound-protein coverage), and fraud detection (account-transaction type coverage).
