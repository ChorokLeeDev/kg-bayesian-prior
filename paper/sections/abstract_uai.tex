% Abstract - NeurIPS revision (Blind Spot Exposure framing)

Every major probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) uncertainty method---variational embeddings, ensembles, energy scoring---shares a systematic blind spot: when familiar entities appear in novel relational contexts, these methods report low uncertainty despite having no training evidence. The problem is worse than random failure: among Energy scoring's 100 most confident predictions, 78\%$\pm$3\% have zero training evidence (2.4$\times$ the base rate). This blind spot affects 11--25\% of test queries across seven benchmarks.

We formalize why this happens: any uncertainty derived from standard KG embeddings achieves near-random detection on novel contexts, because embeddings encode semantic similarity but not which relations each entity has seen. This characterization---while intuitive in hindsight---explains why variance-based, ensemble, dropout, and energy methods all exhibit the same failure mode, and why more training or larger models cannot fix it.

On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14/18), a simple coverage check achieves 0.99 AUROC while all baselines collapse to chance under strict evaluation---on these benchmarks, semantic adds no value beyond coverage. On static benchmarks where emerging entities have partial coverage overlap ($\rho > 0$), semantic adds +8--11pp. Crucially, on GDELT with high coverage overlap ($\rho = 0.84$), CAGP fails (0.57 AUROC), revealing that coverage cannot detect role-shift OOD. The practical fix for novel-context OOD is trivial: track entity-relation coverage via hash table ($O(1)$ lookup, ${<}2\%$ overhead). The contribution is the diagnosis---quantifying and explaining a pervasive failure mode that standard evaluation protocols have masked.
