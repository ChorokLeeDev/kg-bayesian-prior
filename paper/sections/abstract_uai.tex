% Abstract for UAI - Uncertainty decomposition focus (condensed for single-column format)

Probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings use learned variances to quantify epistemic uncertainty, but these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}---an entity receives identical uncertainty regardless of relational context. This conflates two distinct OOD phenomena: \emph{emerging entities} (rare, poorly-learned) and \emph{novel relational contexts} (familiar entities in unobserved relationships). We prove that, under empirically validated frequency-matching assumptions, any uncertainty estimator using only entity-level statistics achieves near-random detection on novel contexts, explaining why existing methods score 0.99 AUROC on random corruptions but only 0.38--0.63 on temporal distribution shift. We decompose uncertainty into complementary \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components, and characterize their distinct failure modes across OOD types. The decomposition achieves 0.86--0.99 AUROC on temporal OOD across three static benchmarks and 0.99 on ICEWS14 (a temporal KG with ground-truth timestamps), substantially improving over relation-agnostic baselines (0.38--0.63).
