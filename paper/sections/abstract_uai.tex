% Abstract for UAI - Diagnosis + working CAGP

Probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) embeddings are often relation-agnostic: each entity receives a single posterior variance regardless of the query relation. Under mild assumptions, this design cannot reliably distinguish novel-context out-of-distribution (OOD) triples---where a familiar entity appears in a new relational role---from in-distribution triples.

We decompose temporal OOD into two categories---emerging entities and novel relational contexts---and show that entity-variance and entity-relation-coverage uncertainties have complementary strengths. This yields a mixture decomposition of overall detection performance: each source dominates one subset, and the formula predicts empirical results to within 0.005 on all static benchmarks.

To validate this framework, we construct a minimal combination method, Coverage-Augmented Gaussian Process (CAGP), that pairs normalized entity-variance with a binary coverage signal tracking observed entity-relation pairs. On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14, ICEWS18), CAGP reaches near-ceiling detection performance. On static benchmarks, where coverage defines both OOD labels and the structural detector (creating a definitional coupling discussed in the method section), results validate the decomposition: coverage dominates novel-context detection, while entity-variance closes the gap on emerging entities.

We also show that reparameterization sampling in variational KG scoring is necessary to avoid variance collapse: without it, Kullback-Leibler regularization collapses entity variances to a uniform value, erasing the uncertainty signal. This pitfall applies to any variational KG embedding where the scoring function ignores learned variance parameters.
