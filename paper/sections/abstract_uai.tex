% Abstract - NeurIPS revision (leads with impossibility theorem)

Probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) embeddings assign each entity a single posterior variance regardless of the query relation. We prove that any such \emph{relation-agnostic} uncertainty estimator achieves near-random AUROC on novel relational contexts: familiar entities appearing in unseen relationships. The bound formally covers all variance-based methods; ensemble and energy-based methods do not satisfy the theorem's strict preconditions but empirically exhibit the same failure pattern.

This impossibility result motivates a decomposition of KG uncertainty into two complementary signals with predictable, non-overlapping failure modes: \emph{semantic} (entity variance) uncertainty, effective for emerging entities but provably blind to novel contexts, and \emph{structural} (entity-relation coverage) uncertainty, perfect for novel contexts but imperfect for emerging entities. An exact mixture-AUROC identity predicts overall detection performance within 0.005 on all benchmarks.

To validate the decomposition, we construct CAGP---a minimal weighted combination of both signals. On temporal KGs with ground-truth timestamps (ICEWS14/18), coverage alone drives near-perfect detection (0.99 AUROC); the semantic component adds emerging-entity lift as predicted (coverage overlap $\rho \approx 0$, i.e., most emerging entities lack any training coverage for the query relation). On static benchmarks with higher coverage overlap ($\rho > 0.3$), semantic provides +8--11pp on emerging entities. Under an adversarial strict split removing 58.5\% inverse-relation overlap, CAGP maintains 1.00 AUROC while Energy collapses to chance. In synthetic settings with ambiguous coverage ($\rho = 1$), semantic provides +31pp over structural---an upper bound demonstrating necessity when coverage is uninformative; real-world gains are +8--11pp.
