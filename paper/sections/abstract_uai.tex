% Abstract for UAI - Diagnosis + working CAGP

Probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings assign each entity a single variance regardless of relational context. We prove this is a fundamental limitation: under empirically validated assumptions, \emph{any} relation-agnostic uncertainty estimator achieves near-random detection of novel relational contexts---familiar entities queried in unobserved relationships. This impossibility result explains why existing methods score only 0.38--0.72 AUROC on temporal distribution shift. Our analysis decomposes KG uncertainty into \emph{semantic} (entity variance via reparameterization) and \emph{structural} (binary entity-relation co-occurrence) components and shows they are complementary: semantic variance detects emerging entities while structural coverage detects novel contexts, each failing where the other succeeds. Our model CAGP combines these signals and achieves 0.90--0.97 AUROC across three benchmarks---consistently outperforming coverage alone, with the largest gains on emerging entities (+8--12pp). On ICEWS14, where OOD is defined by ground-truth timestamps, CAGP achieves 0.993 AUROC. The key to realizing complementarity is proper variational inference: reparameterization sampling in the scoring function prevents variance collapse under KL regularization, enabling the semantic signal to contribute meaningfully to the combination.
