Probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) embeddings represent entities as distributions, using learned variances to quantify epistemic uncertainty. We identify a fundamental limitation: these variances are \emph{relation-agnostic}---an entity receives identical uncertainty regardless of relational context, conflating two distinct out-of-distribution (OOD) phenomena: \emph{emerging entities} (rare, poorly-learned) and \emph{novel relational contexts} (familiar entities in unobserved relationships). We prove an impossibility result: any uncertainty estimator using only entity-level statistics achieves near-random OOD detection on novel contexts, explaining why existing methods achieve 0.99 AUROC on random corruptions but only 0.52--0.64 on temporal distribution shift. We formalize uncertainty decomposition into complementary \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components, proving these signals are non-redundant and that any convex combination strictly dominates either alone. Our method (CAGP) combines both signals via learned weights, achieving 0.94--0.99 AUROC on temporal OOD detection across FB15k-237, WN18RR, and YAGO3-10---a 60--80\% relative improvement over relation-agnostic baselines. The framework is architecture-agnostic, improving DistMult, TransE, and ComplEx uniformly.
