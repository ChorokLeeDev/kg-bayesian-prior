% Abstract for UAI - Diagnosis + working CAGP

Probabilistic knowledge graph (KG) embeddings are often relation-agnostic: each entity receives a single posterior variance regardless of the query relation. Under mild assumptions, this design cannot distinguish novel-context out-of-distribution (OOD) triples---where a familiar entity appears in a new relational role---from in-distribution triples.

We decompose temporal out-of-distribution detection into two categories---emerging entities and novel relational contexts---and show that entity-variance and entity-relation-coverage uncertainties are complementary. Their mixture predicts empirical detection performance to within 0.005 on all static benchmarks.

To validate this, we construct Coverage-Augmented Gaussian Process (CAGP), combining normalized entity-variance with a binary coverage signal. On temporal knowledge graphs with ground-truth timestamps, CAGP reaches near-ceiling detection performance; on static benchmarks, coverage dominates novel-context detection while entity-variance closes the gap on emerging entities.

We also show that reparameterization sampling prevents Kullback-Leibler regularization from collapsing entity variances to a uniform value, erasing the uncertainty signal.
