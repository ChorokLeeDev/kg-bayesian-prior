% Abstract for UAI - Diagnosis + working CAGP

Probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings assign each entity a single variance regardless of relational context. We prove this is a fundamental limitation: under empirically validated assumptions, any relation-agnostic uncertainty estimator achieves near-random detection of novel relational contexts---familiar entities queried in unobserved relationships. Our analysis decomposes KG uncertainty into \emph{semantic} (entity variance) and \emph{structural} (entity-relation co-occurrence) components and proves they are complementary: the overall AUROC decomposes exactly as $\pi_e A_{\text{emerge}} + \pi_n A_{\text{novel}}$, and each signal dominates on one sub-population. Our model CAGP combines both signals, achieving 0.79--0.91 AUROC on emerging entities---the non-trivial detection task---and 0.90--0.97 overall across three benchmarks, improving over coverage alone by +8--12pp on emerging entities. On ICEWS14 with ground-truth temporal splits, CAGP achieves 0.993 AUROC. Realizing this complementarity requires reparameterization sampling to prevent variance collapse under KL regularization.
