% Abstract for EMNLP - Stronger NLP motivation

Knowledge graphs (KGs) underpin critical NLP applications including question answering, dialogue systems, and fact verification.
When these systems encounter queries about unfamiliar entities or novel relational contexts, they should \emph{abstain} rather than hallucinate---yet existing uncertainty methods fail to detect such out-of-distribution (OOD) queries.
We identify a fundamental limitation: probabilistic KG embeddings learn \emph{relation-agnostic} entity variances, missing structural patterns about which entity-relation pairs were observed during training.

We propose a semantic-structural decomposition framework that separates two orthogonal uncertainty types: \emph{semantic uncertainty} from learned embeddings and \emph{structural uncertainty} from entity-relation coverage.
Our analysis reveals complementary failure modes: on emerging entities, learned variance outperforms coverage (0.83 vs 0.78 AUROC); on novel relational contexts, coverage achieves perfect detection while learned variance fails (1.00 vs 0.42).
Combining both signals achieves 0.87--0.96 AUROC across three benchmarks.

On a downstream QA abstention task, our method reduces incorrect answers by 34\% while maintaining 85\% coverage, demonstrating practical value for KG-enhanced NLP systems.
We release code and evaluation protocols to facilitate reproducible research on uncertainty-aware knowledge graph applications.
