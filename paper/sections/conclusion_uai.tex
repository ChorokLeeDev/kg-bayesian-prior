% Conclusion - NeurIPS revision (impossibility-first framing)

We prove that no entity-level uncertainty estimator can detect novel relational contexts in knowledge graphs (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}). This impossibility result explains why all existing methods---variational embeddings, ensembles, energy scoring---fail on temporal OOD detection: they aggregate entity-level statistics that are fundamentally blind to relational structure.

The strongest evidence comes from ICEWS14/18 with ground-truth timestamps: structural coverage alone achieves 0.99 AUROC while baselines collapse to chance under a strict split. Crucially, the semantic component provides \emph{no gain} on these non-circular benchmarks---exactly as the theorem predicts. This is not a limitation but a confirmation: entity-level uncertainty \emph{cannot} detect novel relational contexts, so coverage must do all the work. The mixture-AUROC decomposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity}) predicts this behavior quantitatively, matching observed performance within 0.005 on all benchmarks.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
(1)~\emph{Track entity-relation coverage}: a zero-cost hash table lookup catches the failure mode invisible to all entity-level uncertainty methods. This is the single highest-impact recommendation. (2)~\emph{Use reparameterization sampling} for variational KG embeddings; without it, KL regularization collapses variances and the semantic signal degrades by ${\sim}$20pp (AUROC 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR). (3)~\emph{Report per-category OOD metrics} (emerging vs.\ novel contexts), not just overall AUROC---the impossibility theorem shows that aggregate numbers conceal fundamental failure modes.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
CAGP validates the decomposition; the primary practical contribution is \emph{zero-cost coverage tracking}, which can augment any existing KG system without retraining. The semantic component's failure to improve over coverage on ICEWS14/18 is the \emph{expected outcome} when $\rho \approx 0$---confirming, not contradicting, the theory. On benchmarks with $\rho > 0$, semantic provides +8--11pp gains (real) or +31pp (synthetic upper bound). The contribution is threefold: (1) the impossibility theorem explaining why entity-level methods fail, (2) the decomposition predicting per-category performance, and (3) the practical recommendation to always track entity-relation coverage at zero cost.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
On both non-circular temporal benchmarks (ICEWS14/18), the semantic component provides negligible gains over coverage alone, as predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:info_decomp} since coverage overlap $\rho \approx 0$ (68\% of emerging entities are absent from training). Complementarity between semantic and structural signals is demonstrated only on static benchmarks where $\rho > 0$; in synthetic settings with ambiguous coverage (emerging entities partially covered), semantic provides +31pp over structural, confirming the semantic component \emph{is necessary when $\rho > 0$}. The approach assumes transductive settings; inductive KGs (where entities have no training triples) require different mechanisms. Assumption A4 of Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity} is violated on all benchmarks ($\Delta \geq 1.0$), so formal guarantees are directional rather than worst-case. FB15k-237 and ICEWS14 use 10 seeds; WN18RR and ICEWS18 use 5 seeds; we report standard deviations but do not claim significance for all comparisons.

Future directions include GNN encoders with relation-aware variance, evaluation on additional temporal KGs, adaptive per-relation mixing ($\alpha_r$), and inductive extensions. The decomposition framework \emph{may} generalize beyond KGs: in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), semantic uncertainty (embedding similarity) is similarly blind to whether a query-document pair was seen during training, while structural uncertainty (retrieval co-occurrence) could capture this directly---validation on real RAG benchmarks remains future work.
