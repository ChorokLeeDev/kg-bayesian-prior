We identified a fundamental limitation in probabilistic knowledge graph embeddings: learned variances are relation-agnostic and cannot capture structural uncertainty about entity-relation co-occurrence.
This explains systematic failures of existing uncertainty methods under temporal distribution shift, where they achieve near-random performance despite strong results on artificial benchmarks.

\paragraph{Theoretical Contribution.}
We formalized two distinct OOD types (emerging entities, novel contexts) and proved they require complementary detection strategies (Theorem~\ref{thm:complementarity}).
Semantic uncertainty from embedding variance detects emerging entities; structural uncertainty from coverage detects novel contexts.
Neither alone suffices; combination strictly dominates.

\paragraph{Empirical Findings.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Score-based methods achieving 0.99 AUROC on random corruptions drop to 0.52--0.58 on temporal OOD
    \item Our decomposition achieves 0.87--0.97 AUROC across four benchmarks including ICEWS14 with ground-truth temporal splits
    \item Selective prediction reduces errors by 48\% at 85\% answer rate
    \item The framework generalizes across DistMult, TransE, and ComplEx architectures
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Practical Implications.}
The decomposition is computationally lightweight: coverage is precomputed once from training triples, adding negligible overhead.
For practitioners deploying KG embeddings, augmenting any probabilistic embedding with structural uncertainty provides substantial robustness gains with minimal implementation effort.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Coverage is binary (observed/not), missing gradations between entities seen once versus frequently.
The framework assumes transductive settings; inductive scenarios with truly novel entities require different approaches.
Our base model underperforms state-of-the-art link prediction; stronger base models may exhibit different uncertainty characteristics.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Extensions include: calibration for downstream decision-making; continuous relaxations of binary coverage; integration with inductive KG methods; and application to knowledge-grounded language models where hallucination detection is critical.
