% Conclusion - NeurIPS revision (Blind Spot Exposure framing)

We identify a systematic blind spot in all existing KG uncertainty methods: 11--25\% of test queries involve novel relational contexts where these methods report low uncertainty despite having no training evidence. We prove this failure is unavoidable for relation-agnostic estimators (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}) and show all major methods---variational embeddings, ensembles, energy scoring---exhibit this pattern empirically.

The diagnosis is the contribution. The blind spot has persisted because (1) standard benchmarks leak inverse relations, (2) aggregate AUROC masks per-category failures, and (3) novel-context detection was never separately measured. On ICEWS14/18/GDELT with ground-truth timestamps, all baselines collapse to chance while a simple coverage check achieves 0.99 AUROC. Crucially, on GDELT with high coverage overlap ($\rho = 0.84$), semantic still achieves random AUROC (0.50)---confirming the impossibility is not an artifact of low $\rho$.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
(1)~\emph{Track entity-relation coverage}: a zero-cost hash table lookup catches the failure mode invisible to all entity-level uncertainty methods. This is the single highest-impact recommendation. (2)~\emph{Use reparameterization sampling} for variational KG embeddings; without it, KL regularization collapses variances and the semantic signal degrades by ${\sim}$20pp (AUROC 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR). (3)~\emph{Report per-category OOD metrics} (emerging vs.\ novel contexts), not just overall AUROC---the impossibility theorem shows that aggregate numbers conceal fundamental failure modes.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
CAGP validates the decomposition; the primary practical contribution is \emph{zero-cost coverage tracking}, which can augment any existing KG system without retraining. ICEWS14/18 are \emph{solved cases}: coverage alone achieves 0.99 AUROC, so the semantic component provides zero additional gain---exactly as Empirical Regularity~1 predicts when $\rho \approx 0$. This confirms the theory: we correctly identify \emph{when} each signal contributes. On benchmarks with $\rho > 0$ (FB15k-237, WN18RR), semantic provides +8--11pp; in synthetic settings with $\rho = 1$, the gain reaches +31pp. We show that relation-conditioned variance \emph{can} achieve +25pp on role-shift OOD, but only with explicit auxiliary supervision encoding inverse frequency. Five alternative objectives (binary coverage, contrastive, reconstruction, KL, extended training) all fail to escape this circularity---role-shift OOD is defined by frequency, so non-circular detection is fundamentally impossible for this OOD type. The contribution is fourfold: (1) identifying a pervasive failure mode affecting 11--25\% of queries, (2) proving why it is unavoidable for embedding-based methods (Theorem~\ref{thm:embedding_impossibility}), (3) the decomposition predicting when semantic vs.\ structural signals dominate, and (4) the practical recommendation to always track coverage---a zero-cost fix that should be standard practice.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
ICEWS14/18 are solved cases where coverage suffices ($\rho \approx 0$). GDELT has high $\rho = 0.84$, but semantic still fails because role-shift OOD requires relation-conditioned uncertainty, not entity-level variance. Semantic gains (+8--11pp) appear on static benchmarks (FB15k-237, WN18RR) where the OOD partition creates emerging entities with partial coverage. The approach assumes transductive settings; inductive KGs require different mechanisms. The idealized formalization in Appendix~\ref{app:proof} assumes A4 (bounded OOD prevalence), which is violated on all benchmarks ($\Delta \geq 1.0$); Empirical Regularity~1 is thus an observed pattern rather than a worst-case guarantee. FB15k-237 and ICEWS14 use 10 seeds; WN18RR, ICEWS18, and GDELT use 3--5 seeds.

Future directions include GNN encoders with relation-aware variance, evaluation on additional temporal KGs, adaptive per-relation mixing ($\alpha_r$), and inductive extensions.
