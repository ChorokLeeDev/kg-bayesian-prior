The central insight of this work is that OOD detection in knowledge graphs requires distinguishing \emph{what kind} of distribution shift has occurred---a distinction invisible to relation-agnostic uncertainty estimators.
Our impossibility theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility}) explains \emph{why} all existing entity-level methods fail on novel relational contexts, and the complementarity proposition predicts \emph{how} semantic and structural signals compensate for each other's failure modes.

The strongest non-circular evidence comes from ICEWS14/18 with ground-truth timestamps: structural coverage alone achieves 0.99 AUROC while baselines collapse to chance under a strict split. The theoretical framework explains \emph{why} this is not a coincidence---the impossibility theorem shows that no entity-level method can detect novel relational contexts, making structural coverage a necessary complement. On static benchmarks, the mixture-AUROC decomposition predicts per-category performance within 0.005, providing quantitative evidence that the theory captures the correct structure. The practical recommendation---always track entity-relation coverage alongside embedding uncertainty---follows directly from the theorem, at zero additional cost.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
(1)~\emph{Track entity-relation coverage}: a zero-cost hash table lookup catches the failure mode invisible to all entity-level uncertainty methods. This is the single highest-impact recommendation. (2)~\emph{Use reparameterization sampling} for variational KG embeddings; without it, KL regularization collapses variances and the semantic signal degrades by ${\sim}$20pp (AUROC 0.92$\to$0.72 on WN18RR). (3)~\emph{Report per-category OOD metrics} (emerging vs.\ novel contexts), not just overall AUROC---the impossibility theorem shows that aggregate numbers conceal fundamental failure modes.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
CAGP is not a new KG embedding architecture---it is a diagnostic framework validating that structural uncertainty is necessary. We do not claim the semantic component provides meaningful gains on non-circular temporal benchmarks (it does not; Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}). The contribution is the \emph{impossibility theorem} explaining why all entity-level methods fail, the \emph{decomposition} predicting per-category performance, and the \emph{practical recommendation} to always track entity-relation coverage---which requires zero additional compute.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
On both non-circular temporal benchmarks (ICEWS14/18), the semantic component provides negligible gains over coverage alone; under the strict split, $U_{\text{sem}}$ actually degrades (0.78$\to$0.69 on ICEWS14), confirming relation-agnostic variance is fundamentally insufficient for novel-context detection. Complementarity between semantic and structural signals is demonstrated only on static benchmarks where novel-context detection is circular (by construction). Additional temporal KGs are needed to find a setting where the semantic component is \emph{necessary} for strong non-circular OOD detection. The approach assumes transductive settings; inductive KGs (where entities have no training triples) require different mechanisms. Assumption A4 of Proposition~\ref{prop:complementarity} is violated on all benchmarks ($\Delta \geq 1.0$), so formal guarantees are directional rather than worst-case. FB15k-237 and ICEWS14 use 10 seeds; WN18RR and ICEWS18 use 5 seeds; we report standard deviations but do not claim significance for all comparisons.

Future directions include GNN encoders with relation-aware variance, evaluation on additional temporal KGs, adaptive per-relation mixing ($\alpha_r$), inductive extensions, and application to retrieval-augmented generation.
