The central insight of this work is that OOD detection in knowledge graphs requires distinguishing \emph{what kind} of distribution shift has occurred---a distinction invisible to relation-agnostic uncertainty estimators.
By decomposing uncertainty into semantic and structural components, CAGP combines their complementary strengths: semantic variance captures emerging entities while structural coverage captures novel relational contexts, each compensating where the other fails.
Empirically, CAGP achieves 0.90--0.97 AUROC across three static benchmarks and 0.993 on ICEWS14 (Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ood}), consistently outperforming both coverage alone and all baselines. The improvement is largest on emerging entities (+8--12pp), confirming that the semantic signal provides genuine complementary value when properly trained.
The decomposition is architecture-agnostic (DistMult, TransE, ComplEx; Appendix~\ref{app:architectures}) and computationally lightweight (coverage is precomputed once).
These properties make it applicable to domains where KG reliability is critical, such as drug interaction prediction, clinical decision support, and recommender systems.

\paragraph{Scalability considerations.}
CAGP's coverage matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{E}| \times |\mathcal{R}|}$ requires $O(|\mathcal{E}| \times |\mathcal{R}|)$ memory. For our evaluated datasets (FB15k-237: 13MB dense, $<$1MB sparse; YAGO3-10: 17.5MB dense, $<$1MB sparse), this is negligible. For web-scale KGs, sparse storage (hash tables storing only observed pairs) reduces memory to $O(|\mathcal{T}|)$ with $O(1)$ inference. We also explored learned relation-conditioned variance via multi-layer perceptron, but found explicit coverage simpler and equally effective. CAGP provides interpretability and simplicity suitable for domain-specific KGs with $<$1M entities; empirical evaluation on billion-triple KGs remains future work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
On simulated splits, structural AUROC $= 1$ on novel contexts is by construction (Remark~\ref{rem:circularity}); ICEWS14 breaks this coupling but is a single temporal KG---evaluation on additional temporal benchmarks would strengthen generalizability. We tested bilinear scoring as a step toward relation-aware architectures (Appendix~\ref{app:bilinear}); full message-passing models (R-GCN, CompGCN) remain untested.
CAGP uses fixed equal weighting ($\alpha{=}0.5$), which is near-optimal across all datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity}). The robustness to $\alpha$ reflects the complementary nature of the signals; an adaptive mechanism for datasets with extreme OOD compositions remains future work.
Reparameterization sampling is essential: without it, KL regularization collapses entity variances (see error analysis), a pitfall that likely affects other probabilistic KG embedding methods.
Coverage is binary; the approach assumes transductive settings; assumptions A4 and A6 are violated on some datasets, so formal guarantees should be interpreted qualitatively.
The decomposition supports selective prediction, with preliminary results showing up to 43\% error reduction at 85\% coverage (Appendix~\ref{app:selective}).

\paragraph{Future directions.}
(1)~\emph{GNN encoders.} We tested relation-conditioned bilinear scoring ($h^\top W_r t$ with basis decomposition; Appendix~\ref{app:bilinear}), which does not improve entity-level OOD detection. The open question is whether full message-passing architectures (R-GCN~\citep{schlichtkrull2018modeling}, CompGCN~\citep{vashishth2019composition}) produce relation-aware entity variance through neighborhood aggregation.
(2)~\emph{Adaptive mixing.} The flat $\alpha$ landscape (Appendix~\ref{app:alpha_sensitivity}) reflects non-overlapping failure modes; per-relation $\alpha_r$ could benefit datasets with heterogeneous signal strengths across relations.
(3)~\emph{Inductive settings.} Coverage is undefined for entities absent from training; hybrid approaches combining graph-structural features with coverage may extend CAGP to inductive KG completion.
(4)~\emph{Scalability.} Sparse coverage storage and streaming updates would enable deployment on evolving, web-scale KGs.
(5)~\emph{Applications.} In retrieval-augmented generation, $U_{\text{str}}$ can flag queries lacking relational grounding while $U_{\text{sem}}$ flags poorly-constrained entities, enabling targeted abstention.
