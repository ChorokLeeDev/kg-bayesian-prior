Knowledge Graph Embedding (KGE) methods achieve impressive link prediction accuracy but provide only point estimates.
For safety-critical applications---medical KGs predicting drug interactions, financial KGs for fraud detection---knowing \emph{when predictions are unreliable} is essential.

Existing uncertainty approaches fall short.
Triple-level methods~\citep{chen2019embedding} cannot identify which entities are poorly understood.
Post-hoc methods like MC Dropout often yield poorly calibrated uncertainties.
GP-based methods~\citep{chen2022graph} incorporate graph structure but have not evaluated whether their uncertainties are meaningful.

A key observation motivates our approach: existing GP methods treat all relations identically.
But knowledge graphs are heterogeneous---\texttt{born\_in} implies different similarity patterns than \texttt{similar\_to}.
Entities sharing a birthplace may otherwise be unrelated, while \texttt{similar\_to} suggests global similarity.

We propose \ours{}, a Gaussian Process prior with a \textbf{relation-aware kernel} that assigns learnable parameters per relation type:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lengthscale} $\ell_r$: how far information propagates along relation $r$
    \item \textbf{Variance} $\sigma_r^2$: how much relation $r$ contributes to similarity
\end{itemize}

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Relation-aware GP kernel} capturing heterogeneous KG structure
    \item \textbf{+55\% OOD detection} on FB15k-237, +34\% on YAGO3-10 vs deterministic baselines
    \item \textbf{Relation density threshold}: \ours{} requires $\geq$30 relation types to be effective
    \item \textbf{Discovery}: GGPN~\citep{chen2022graph} is miscalibrated (AUROC$<$0.5)
\end{enumerate}
